01 · Summary

The short answer

Gold and silver fell for two reasons. The Fed turned from cutting rates to hiking them, and leveraged positions were forced to sell into that turn. Everything else made the fall bigger or smaller.

  • The Fed is cause number one. Each large down-leg lines up with a Fed event: Kevin Warsh named as the next chair (30 Jan), a hawkish first meeting (17 Jun), the first hike since 2023 (16 Sep), then hot CPI and hawkish minutes (10 and 18 Nov). A December hike is about 97% priced. The 10-year US real yield cleared at 2.903% on 19 Nov, the highest since 2002.
  • Leverage turned a correction into a crash. CME raised silver margins from 9% to 18% of contract value in nine days around the January top, and gold from 6% to 8%. MCX's effective silver margin reached about 72%. In November, margin calls from the AI-stock rout forced investors to sell gold for cash.
  • The war did not help gold. The Iran war pushed oil and inflation up, which meant more rate hikes. The war began on 28 Feb; gold fell 12% in March and rose when a ceasefire was agreed on 8 Apr.
  • Silver fell almost twice as hard (−67% against −37%). Solar makers cut silver use by about 19%, the 2025 London squeeze and the US tariff premium unwound, and speculators left.
  • Indians lost less. MCX gold is about 33% off its peak, and IBJA 24K gold is down about 9% for 2026, against about 18% in dollars. The reason is the 13 May import-duty hike from 6% to 15%. If a Budget cuts the duty back, rupee prices fall about 7.8% overnight.
  • The losses are concentrated. They sit with 2026 buyers (a record ₹24,040 crore went into gold ETFs in the peak month), leveraged MCX traders and gold-loan borrowers at maximum loan-to-value. End-2024 buyers are still up about 58%, and 2020–21 Sovereign Gold Bonds about 163% before tax.

03 · Causes, ranked by evidence

The Fed's U-turn ranks first, and forced selling did the damage

The ranking weighs three things: timing against the price falls, the reasons banks and the World Gold Council gave, and the counter-evidence. It is a judgement from that evidence. No source offered a formal breakdown of the whole fall.

    AgainstWGC says falling yields supported gold in October; on 20 Nov the real yield fell 11bp while gold still fell 3–4%.

  2. 2

    Leverage and forced liquidation turned each Fed shock into a crash

    Strong for the sharpest days
    • CME raised silver margins in late December (about $22k → $25k a contract), moved to percentage-of-value margins around 13 Jan, then raised silver 9% → 11% (28 Jan), 11% → 15% and gold 6% → 8% (effective 2 Feb), and silver 15% → 18% (6 Feb). The widely quoted "five hikes in nine days" could not be verified as a count.
    • MCX: margins of 20% (gold) and 25% (silver) from 1 Feb plus extra margins on 5–6 Feb. ICICI Securities put effective silver margin at about 72% and gold at 30% until 19 Feb BT.
    • March: a record $12bn gold-ETF outflow. WGC: investors were "selling what they could rather than what they wanted to be rid of".
    • November: the AI-stock rout. Nvidia fell 14.01% on 20 Nov. Gold ETFs lost 20.4t (~$2.2bn) in the week to 20 Nov, the most since June, led by North America (11.8t); SPDR GLD fell to 1,034.4t FXEmpire/WGC. Gold and the S&P 500 each fell 8.8% from 30 Oct to 20 Nov. ING: "liquidation, not rates alone, was doing the damage" view.
    • CME raised margins again after the slide (advisory 26-389, 23 Nov, effective after the 24 Nov close): gold 5% → 6%, silver 8% → 9% Invezz.

    AgainstMargin hikes mostly came after volatility rose. Gold speculative longs (102,942 contracts on 17 Nov) are 29% below the August peak but still near the five-year median, so the washout may not be complete.

  3. 3

    The Iran war hurt gold, because oil meant inflation and rate hikes

    Moderate to strong
    • US-Israeli strikes on Iran began on Sat 28 Feb. Gold rose only 0.4% to $5,297 on the first trading day (2 Mar), then fell more than 6% on 3 Mar CNBC. Gold fell 12% in March, moving opposite to oil Forbes.
    • Brent futures peaked at $126.41 on 30 Apr CNN. The conflict is not over: the ceasefire collapsed in July, and on 24 Nov Iran said it would keep the Strait of Hormuz closed "day by day".
    • The 8 Apr US-Iran ceasefire lifted gold about 2% to $4,790, because fears of energy-driven inflation eased.
    • Central banks were net sellers of 30t in March (37t bought, 66t sold), the first net-selling month after ten months of buying. Turkey sold about 60t; Russia 6t per WGC data 16t in one WGC statement.

    AgainstOne Indian view says falling oil and easing tensions in November moved money into equities view. In 2025, easing tensions (US-China truce, Ukraine talks) triggered 4.5–6% falls.

  4. 4

    A firmer dollar

    Moderate, secondary
    • Dollar index from about 100.5 (June) to 103.41 on 13 Nov and 104.54 on 23 Nov, a 19–20-month high.

    AgainstThat is about +3% against gold's −35%. On 24 Nov gold fell even as the dollar eased.

  5. 5

    Jewellery and coin buyers left before the crash

    Weak as a trigger; removed a cushion
    • WGC Q3 2026: global jewellery −17% to 313t; bars and coins −19% to 268t WGC.
    • China's Q1 jewellery demand −32% after a VAT exemption ended on 1 Nov 2025; India's Q3 demand −29%.

    AgainstTotal global demand fell only 1% in Q3, because ETFs (+211t) and central banks took up the slack. Indian buyers came back as prices fell.

Not a cause: central banks

Central banks bought 321t in Q3 2026, up 42%, against a five-year quarterly average of 227t. September was the strongest buying month of 2026 at +61t. China's central bank added 25.8t in October, its 24th straight month and about 129t so far this year. RBI held at 880.52t all year and denied a report that it sold $12bn of gold; falling prices cut the value of its gold to $98.61bn (14.0% of reserves) by 13 Nov. Goldman calls central-bank buying of about 60t a month the main support for the price view. WGC

Unproven: India's "buying strike"

PM Modi said Indians who followed his 10 May appeal "saved money", and Piyush Goyal said "Indians influenced global gold prices" government claim. India's October imports did fall 62.6%. But the big falls line up with Fed dates, and global demand barely moved. The evidence does not show India moved the world price. The Week

Where analysts disagree
  • What drove November. ING and Peter Schiff point to liquidation and margin calls; Goldman, JPMorgan and Modern Diplomacy point to the Fed and real rates.
  • What drove October. WGC's model says yields supported gold, so it blames "asset rotation, de-grossing and Fed rate-path repricing".
  • Is the bottom close? UBS calls the sell-off "positioning-led, not structural"; WGC says gold "may be nearing an interim bottom". Goldman warns ETF holders could give up below $3,890 (spot is already below it); Saxo's Ole Hansen says the sell-off "may not be over".
  • Silver on 30 Jan. Some blame Warsh and the dollar, others CME margin mechanics.
04 · Silver

Silver fell twice as far because its shortage story collapsed

From 30 Oct to 20 Nov silver fell 15.4%, exactly as much as copper, while gold fell 8.8%. In this leg silver traded like an industrial metal, not like money. BullionVault

The shortage premium disappeared

In October 2025 London ran short of free silver: one-month lease rates hit about 39% against a normal level below 1%, after about 225 Moz moved to New York vaults on tariff fears. Lease rates were back to 2–3% by mid-Q1 2026. By end-September London held 932 Moz, the most in nearly four years. Kitco

The tariff premium came out

Silver joined the US Critical Minerals List on 7 Nov 2025, opening the door to Section 232 tariffs. On 14–15 Jan 2026 Trump held off on tariffs, and silver fell more than 7% from above $93 in a day. No public outcome was found after the review deadline of about 13 Jul: no tariff, stockpile or price floor for silver has been announced. China has required export licences for silver since 1 Jan 2026 (44 firms approved for 2026–27). ING

High prices destroyed demand

The Silver Institute cut its 2026 deficit forecast from 46.3 Moz (April) to 35 Moz (12 Nov), the narrowest in six years. Total demand −5% to 1.08bn oz. Solar use is forecast to fall about 19% to roughly 151 Moz as makers use less silver per panel and switch to copper. Non-solar industrial demand still rose 4.5% to 492 Moz. Silver Institute

Speculators left

On 30 Jan the largest silver ETF (SLV) lost 31% and leveraged silver ETFs more than 60%. Global silver ETF holdings fell 55 Moz in 2026. Mint coin sales fell more than 80% from Q1 to Q2. COMEX speculative net longs were down to about 1,280 contracts on 17 Nov, so speculators have almost fully left. Business Quant

India's own silver twist

DGFT Notification 17/2026-27 (16 May) moved silver bars from "Free" to "Restricted"; grain and powder followed in June. May imports fell about 94% conflict on tonnes, and domestic premiums hit a six-month high by July. DGFT announced faster licence approvals on 13 Oct, and on 22 Oct Reuters reported, citing four dealers, that permits for about 6,500t over six months had been issued (Bloomberg: up to 6,000t). That removed the scarcity premium. MCX silver slid from ₹1,79,132/kg at end-October to ₹1,48,900 on 21 Nov.

2027 is disputed. Metals Focus expects a physical surplus in 2027; Bank of America expects a deficit views. A 35 Moz deficit is only about 3% of demand, and existing stockpiles cover it calc.

The duty, not the rupee, explains most of the gap

On 13 May 2026 India raised the import duty on gold and silver from 6% to 15%: basic customs duty 5% → 10% and the agriculture cess (AIDC) 1% → 5%, with 3% IGST on top (Customs Notifications 15/2026 and 16/2026, dated 12 May). It came two days after PM Modi, at a rally in Hyderabad on 10 May, asked Indians not to buy gold jewellery for a year. MCX gold jumped 7.2% that day. No rollback has been announced; the government cut the tariff value to $1,149 per 10 g from 24 Nov (Notification 96/2026) as world prices fell. CNBC TaxGuru

Hike: 1.15 ÷ 1.06 = 1.085 → +8.5% on landed cost Rollback: 1.06 ÷ 1.15 = 0.922 → −7.8% overnight

The rupee was 91.99 on 29 Jan and 92.62 on 20 Nov, so it explains little of the gap since the peak. The duty hike also revived smuggling: dealers told Reuters illegal imports could exceed 100t in 2026, against 20.4t in 2025.

Assets fell even as money kept coming in

New money into gold FoFs was stopped on SEBI's instruction

From 16 Oct, about 17 fund houses (including HDFC, ICICI Prudential, Tata, Invesco and Kotak) stopped fresh lump sums, new SIPs/STPs and switch-ins to gold ETF fund-of-funds, as instructed by SEBI. Gold loans grew far faster than gold prices

Bank gold loans were ₹5.52 lakh crore on 31 Jul 2026, up 88.1% (RBI data via Outlook Money); CRIF puts the whole system, including NBFCs, at ₹23.3 lakh crore, up 60.6%. Muthoot's Stage 2 loans rose to about ₹23,467 crore from ₹1,876 crore; lenders expect more auctions in the December quarter.

SGBs: long-term holders still win, but early redemption is now taxed

No new Sovereign Gold Bond since February 2024. RBI set ₹12,612 for the 25 Nov early redemption of 2021-22 Series I (+167%), 6.6% below the ₹13,499 set a week earlier for Series VIII. From 1 Apr 2026 the capital-gains exemption covers only bonds "subscribed to at the time of original issue and held continuously until redemption on maturity". Early redemptions like these, and exchange purchases even held to maturity, are taxed at 12.5%. Taxscan Angel One

Listed companies took the hit too

On 18 Nov Titan fell about 4.4%, Kalyan 4.7%, and Muthoot and Manappuram about 5% each (Muthoot at a 52-week low of ₹2,305). On 20 Nov Kalyan fell about 7% and Senco about 2%.

08 · Playbook

Set a target allocation, buy in stages, avoid leverage

This is general evidence, not personal advice. Anyone with specific needs should speak to a SEBI-registered investment adviser. Two reasons to hold some gold survived the crash: central banks are buying at a record pace, and a falling rupee has cushioned Indian buyers in past slumps.

  • Target allocationGold 5–10% (up to 15%); silver 0–3%
  • Buy in stagesMonthly SIPs or pre-set price steps
  • No leverageMargins rise exactly when prices fall
  • Regulated, low-costETFs, FoFs, multi-asset funds
  • Tax before sellingShort-term gains taxed at slab rate
How the professionals are buying

DSP's Multi Asset Omni FoF held about 10% gold and no silver against a 20% benchmark weight in the two metals. On 18 Nov it said it would add 0.5% to gold from around $3,850, and 0.5% to silver from just below $47 and at every further $2 fall. It calls gold 15% and silver 28% below fair value view. Motilal Oswal advises gradual buying in the $3,800–3,900 zone view. The same fund house was "cautious on gold, bullish on silver" in October 2025, the opposite of what happened, so treat fund-house calls as views. BT CNBC-TV18

Before deciding you are under-allocated, count the jewellery you already own. Rebalance once a year in both directions: trim after rallies, top up after falls.

What each kind of investor can reasonably do

2026 buyer of gold ETFs or FoFs, now down 20–35%

Do not sell in a panic. If gold is still within your target range, keep existing SIPs running.

Selling locks in the loss. The rupee and the duty already softened the fall, and most 2027 base cases sit above today's price view.

Long-term holder in profit (pre-2025, 2020–21 SGBs)

Stick to the plan. Trim only if gold has grown well above your target range.

Below target, with a long horizon

Build up over several months through monthly SIPs or pre-set price steps, using an ETF, FoF or multi-asset fund.

In 2011–15 a first fall of 30% became 45%. DSP and Motilal both describe buying in stages.

Buying for a wedding

Buy what you need, in parts across the season, and check the 6-digit HUID on the BIS CARE app.

Making charges of 8–25% plus 3% GST are lost on resale, so jewellery is consumption, not investment.

Holding silver bought near the top

Treat it as a long-term, high-risk position. Size it so a further 30–40% fall would not change your plans; do not add beyond a small tactical share.

Even bullish 2027 forecasts ($84–90) are far below $121.64. Silver fell 39% in the 13 weeks to 24 Nov.

Leveraged MCX trader

Reduce position size and keep cash for margin hikes.

Gold-loan borrower at high LTV

Check your current LTV now. Keep cash ready for a top-up or part-payment. Avoid bullet loans you cannot repay within 12 months.

Mistakes the 2026 crash has already punished

  1. Selling in a panic at the low. It locks in a loss the rupee and duty already softened. Ask "am I above or below my target allocation?" instead.
  2. Averaging down with leverage on MCX. Margins went up exactly when prices fell, forcing traders out whatever their view.
  3. Buying the dip in one lump sum. History shows a 30% fall can become 45% and last for years.
  4. Overweighting silver because it "fell more". The bigger fall reflects bigger risk, and the shortage story behind it has weakened.
  5. Treating jewellery or app-based digital gold as an investment, and forgetting the jewellery you already own when sizing your gold holding.
  6. Trusting confident forecasts. In January ING said silver's "downside might be limited"; on 18 Nov a headline said silver had found "a $50 floor" with silver already near $45.
  7. Assuming the 15% duty is permanent, or buying SGBs on the exchange expecting a tax-free maturity.
  8. Borrowing against gold at maximum LTV near a price peak.
