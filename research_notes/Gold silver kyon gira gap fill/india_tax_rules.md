## 1. Sovereign Gold Bonds: Budget 2026 tax change, premature redemption, exchange buyers, new issues

### Takeaway
**CONFIRMED:** Budget 2026 (Finance Bill 2026) limits the tax-free redemption to bonds **subscribed at original issue and held continuously until redemption on maturity**, effective 1 Apr 2026. Exchange buyers pay tax even at maturity. **LIKELY:** premature redemption through the RBI, from the 5th year, is now taxable **even for original subscribers**. This is stricter than the brief's "possibly". No new SGB has been issued since Feb 2024.

### Cited Findings
- Per the Memorandum to Finance Bill 2026, as reported, the exemption is available only where the bond "is subscribed to at the time of original issue and held continuously until redemption on maturity", from 1 Apr 2026. Secondary-market buyers lose it. — [Taxscan](https://www.taxscan.in/top-stories/sovereign-gold-bond-tax-rules-for-itr-ay-2026-27-interest-redemption-capital-gains-and-reporting-1448954); [ClearTax](https://cleartax.in/s/capital-gain-tax-on-sovereign-gold-bond-sgb); [Upstox](https://upstox.com/news/personal-finance/tax/new-sovereign-gold-bond-rule-2026-capital-gains-tax-exemption-only-on-original-issue-held-till-maturity/article-188712/); [Value Research](https://www.valueresearchonline.com/learn/mutual-funds/sovereign-gold-bonds-budget-2026-tax-rule-change/); [KPMG India Budget 2026-27 FS POV (PDF)](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmgsites/in/pdf/2026/02/financial-services-pov-union-budget-2026-27.pdf)
- Legal basis: s.70(1)(x) of the Income-tax Act, 2025 (formerly s.47(viic) of the 1961 Act) was amended. BusinessToday (2 Feb 2026) framed it as Budget 2026 "clarifying" the exemption rules. — [Multibagg](https://www.multibagg.ai/market-pulse/articles/budget-2026-sgb-tax-rules-cml3i469c0009qn0jc0q0xw62); [BusinessToday, 2 Feb 2026](https://www.businesstoday.in/personal-finance/investment/story/up-to-39-tax-on-sgb-gains-investors-face-up-tough-tax-trap-after-budget-2026-clarifies-exemption-rules-514178-2026-02-02)

- Premature RBI redemption: before 31 Mar 2026 it was exempt. From 1 Apr 2026 several sources say it is taxable even for original subscribers, because it is not "redemption on maturity". Gains are taxed as 12.5% LTCG. — [Chartered Online](https://charteredonline.in/rbi-announces-sgb-premature-redemption-calendar-for-2026-27-check-all-32-eligible-gold-bond-tranches-and-taxability-thereof/); [Toolisky](https://toolisky.com/blog/sgb-premature-redemption-tax-exemption); [Upstox FAQ](https://upstox.com/news/personal-finance/tax/sovereign-gold-bond-tax-changes-2026-sgb-tax-rate-old-vs-new-rules-applicable-date-facts-and-fa-qs/article-188770/). Sources disagree on fine details, and no CBDT/RBI clarification was found.
- Some sources say the exemption applies only to individuals, while others do not mention an individual-only condition. **Unresolved.** — [Upstox](https://upstox.com/news/personal-finance/tax/new-sovereign-gold-bond-rule-2026-capital-gains-tax-exemption-only-on-original-issue-held-till-maturity/article-188712/)

### Inferences

This matters for the 18 and 25 Nov 2026 redemption windows that the brief highlights (Series VIII +163%, 2021-22 Series I +167%): those gains are taxable. An original subscriber gets the exemption only by holding to the full 8-year maturity.
- SGB interest (2.5% a year) is taxable at slab rate. This is standard law and was not re-verified this round.

## 2. Gold/silver ETFs, FoFs, physical gold: rates, holding periods, ₹1.25 lakh exemption

### Takeaway
**CONFIRMED:**
- **Listed gold/silver ETFs:** long-term after **more than 12 months**, taxed at **12.5% without indexation**. Short-term gains are taxed at slab rate.
- **Gold/silver FoFs (unlisted MF units), physical gold/silver and jewellery:** long-term after **more than 24 months**, taxed at **12.5%**.
- **The ₹1.25 lakh exemption does not apply to gold ETFs:** it covers only equity (old s.112A).

Budget 2026 made no change to ETF/FoF taxation.

### Cited Findings
- Gold, silver and international ETFs qualify for 12.5% LTCG after 12 months because they are exchange-listed. FoFs (unlisted) qualify for 12.5% after 24 months. — [Business Standard, 23 Jul 2024](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/budget/news/budget-2024-brings-relief-for-gold-etfs-equity-fofs-international-schemes-124072301258_1.html); [Business Standard, Sept 2024](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/shorter-ltcg-holding-period-seen-boosting-gold-exchange-traded-fund-inflows-124092001047_1.html); [Business Standard, 10 Oct 2025](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/finance/personal-finance/gold-and-silver-surge-know-how-etfs-are-taxed-before-you-invest-in-them-125101000906_1.html); [Finnovate](https://www.finnovate.in/learn/blog/etf-taxation-india)
- Gold ETFs do **not** get the ₹1.25 lakh exemption, which is equity-only. — [Shriram Finance](https://shriramfinance.in/articles/gold-loan/2026/capital-gains-tax-on-gold); [Finnovate](https://www.finnovate.in/learn/blog/mutual-fund-taxation-india-fy-2025-26)
- Section 50AA: after Budget 2024, a "specified mutual fund" (always short-term, slab rate) means a fund with more than 65% in debt/money-market instruments, or an FoF with 65% or more in such funds. Gold/silver FoFs therefore fall outside it. Sources differ on the effective date (FY2025-26 vs 1 Apr 2025/2026), but all agree it applies for FY2026-27. — [Fincart](https://www.fincart.com/blog/debt-mutual-funds-taxation---meaning-types-stcg-ltcg-rules); [Finnovate](https://www.finnovate.in/learn/blog/mutual-fund-taxation-india-fy-2025-26); [Taxgarden](https://taxgarden.in/blog/mutual-fund-taxation-india-ay-2026-27); [taxguru (50AA amendment)](https://taxguru.in/income-tax/amendment-specified-mutual-fund-definition-section-50aa-budget-2024.html)
- Conflicting outliers: Vested puts gold ETFs at 24 months, and TaxFetch cites the pre-2024 rule of 3 years and 20% with indexation. Both conflict with the majority and look outdated. — [Vested](https://vested.blog/posts/what-is-section-112-long-term-capital-gains); [TaxFetch](https://taxfetchindia.com/blog/investment-guide/gold-etf-mutual-fund-tax-2026)

### Inferences
- The brief's table (ETF 12 months; FoF, jewellery and coins 24 months; 12.5%) is **CONFIRMED**. Its flag "[sources differ on FoF details]" can be removed. Gold FoFs invest about 100% in gold ETF units, not debt, so they are not "specified mutual funds" and take 24 months at 12.5%. This is an inference from the definition. The equity-FoF "90% in ETFs" rule concerns *equity-oriented* FoFs and does not apply to gold.
- The ₹1.25 lakh exemption point is **CONFIRMED** (no exemption for gold ETFs).

### Gaps
- The new Income-tax Act 2025 section numbers for the 12.5% LTCG rule (old s.112) and the holding-period definition were not retrieved.

## 3. Setting off and carrying forward capital losses on gold ETFs

### Takeaway
**UNVERIFIED this round.** No search result directly addressed gold-ETF loss set-off. The standard rules below come from general knowledge of the 1961 Act and have not been checked against the 2025 Act.

### Cited Findings
- No source found specifically. The ETF-tax search returned nothing on set-off — [search covering Finnovate/Shriram/Business Standard](https://www.finnovate.in/learn/blog/etf-taxation-india).

### Inferences
- Standard law, **unverified**:
  - A short-term capital loss can be set off against both short- and long-term capital gains.
  - A long-term capital loss can be set off only against long-term gains.
  - Neither can be set off against salary or other income.
  - Unabsorbed losses carry forward for 8 assessment years, but only if the return is filed by the due date.

  This would let a 2026 buyer who booked a short-term gold-ETF loss offset it against, for example, equity gains. The Income-tax Act 2025 is believed to keep these rules, but this was not verified. **Keep the brief's "CA check" caveat.**

## 5. MCX commodity derivatives taxation

### Takeaway
**LIKELY:** for individuals, MCX bullion futures and options income is **non-speculative business income** (exchange-traded commodity derivatives on which CTT is charged are excluded from "speculative"). It is taxed at slab rate and reported in ITR-3. **CTT on non-agri futures is 0.01% on the sell side.**

### Cited Findings
- Commodity derivatives on a recognised exchange are non-speculative business income under s.43(5) (1961 Act). Profits are added to income at slab rate. CTT paid is deductible as a business expense. Losses carry forward 8 years and can be set off against other income except salary. ITR-3 is used. — [JM Financial](https://www.jmfinancialservices.in/blogs-and-articles/how-to-save-tax-on-commodity-trading-in-india); [Mastertrust](https://mastertrust.co.in/blog/how-commodity-trading-is-taxed-in-india-a-clear-guide-for-mcx-traders); [Quicko Q&A](https://qna.tax/t/is-commodity-trading-considered-as-non-speculative-or-speculative-business-income/9562)
- CTT on non-agricultural futures is 0.01% of turnover on the sell side; agricultural futures are exempt. — [Mastertrust](https://mastertrust.co.in/blog/how-commodity-trading-is-taxed-in-india-a-clear-guide-for-mcx-traders); [Calcguru](https://calcguru.in/fno-intraday-turnover-tax-calculator/)

### Inferences
- Change "Business income [unconfirmed]" in the brief to "non-speculative business income, slab rate [LIKELY]".

## 7. GST

### Takeaway
**CONFIRMED (secondary consensus):** GST is **3%** on gold and silver (bullion, coins, jewellery). For a retail jewellery purchase, 3% applies to the **whole bill, including making charges**. The **5%** rate applies only to **job work** (SAC 9988), for example a karigar's labour on a jeweller's gold. The rate was unchanged by the Sept 2025 GST rationalisation.

### Cited Findings
- 3% applies to the whole value including making charges (composite supply, per the government sectoral FAQ as cited). 5% applies to job work under Entry 26, Notification 11/2017-CT(Rate). — [OroPocket](https://blog.oropocket.com/making-charges-on-gold/); [TaxTMI](https://www.taxtmi.com/article/detailed?id=13416); [Terra Insight](https://www.terra-insight.com/insights/gold-tax-3-percent-hsn-7113-vs-making-charges-5-percent-cbic-notification/); [CaratOS](https://caratos.co.in/guides/gst-billing-for-jewellery-shops/); [BlueStone](https://blog.bluestone.com/gst-on-gold-jewellery-2026/)

### Inferences

## 8. RBI gold-loan rules, monitoring, sector data, auctions

### Takeaway

- The LTV tiers of 85/80/75% are **LIKELY**: confirmed by secondary sources, but the primary text was not retrieved.
- RBI monitoring of loans above 100% LTV is reported, attributed to "sources" in a **Moneycontrol** piece (syndicated on TradingView); the date was not pinned.
- **WRONG-IN-BRIEF:** the "₹3.74 trn, +60–66%" figure is mislabelled. RBI bank data shows ₹5.52 lakh crore (+88.1%) in July 2026. CRIF shows the whole system at ₹23.3 lakh crore (+60.6%).

### Cited Findings
- **The Directions:** issued 6 Jun 2025. They apply to banks, co-operative banks and all NBFCs, with compliance by 1 Apr 2026.
