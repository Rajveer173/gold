## 2. Rupee: USD/INR path in 2025–26 and how much of the rupee gold return it explains

### Takeaway
The rupee was a tailwind for Indian gold in 2025 (about -5%, Asia's worst currency) and into early 2026. It hit a record low of about 96.97 in May 2026. In Nov 2026 the rupee rallied: from 94.19 on 13 Nov to 92.62 on 20 Nov, about +1.7%, on foreign-portfolio-investor (FPI) inflows. That rally made the rupee fall in gold slightly worse than the dollar fall during the crash week.

### Inferences
- 2025: about 5 percentage points of Indian gold and silver returns came from the rupee alone, because USD/INR went from about 85.6 to 89.88. The 85.6 starting level is my approximation and is not sourced here.
- 2026 year to date: from 89.88 to 92.62 the rupee is still about 3% weaker, which cushioned Indian holders against the dollar-price fall by about 3 points.
- Peak (29 Jan, about 92) to now (about 92.6): the rupee is roughly flat, so it neither cushioned nor worsened the fall from the peak.
- Crash week (13 Nov to 20 Nov): the rupee gained about 1.7%, which added roughly 1.7 points to the rupee fall in gold. Of MCX gold's roughly 11% weekly fall, about 1.5–2 points is the rupee effect.
- From the May record low (96.97) to 92.62, the rupee is about 4.7% stronger. For anyone who bought gold in May–Oct 2026, the rupee rally added to losses.

## 3. Duties and taxes: customs duty, AIDC, GST, tariff value

### Takeaway
After the July 2024 cut, the duty was 6% (5% basic duty plus 1% AIDC), and Budget 2026 (1 Feb 2026) left it unchanged. On 13 May 2026 the government raised it to 15% (10% basic duty plus 5% AIDC) to defend the rupee and the current account. It came days after PM Modi's 10 May appeal to stop buying gold for a year. GST stays at 3%. The duty hike lifted domestic prices relative to world prices by about 8.5%, which partly cushioned Indian investors in the fall. The flip side: a future duty cut would knock about 7.8% off domestic prices overnight.

### Cited Findings
- July 2024 Budget cut gold and silver customs duty from 15% to 6% — [Gulf News, Jul 2024](https://gulfnews.com/business/banking/india-budget-2024-custom-duties-on-gold-silver-slashed-down-to-6-from-15-1.1721716047012)
- Budget 2026 (1 Feb 2026) left gold and silver duty unchanged at 6%. LKP's Jateen Trivedi said the lack of a bullion duty update drew a negative market reaction. One outlier source (Aditya Birla Capital) claims a cut to 5%, which conflicts with the others — [NewsBytes](https://www.newsbytesapp.com/news/business/union-budget-2026-no-change-in-gold-silver-import-duties/story); [Fintaxblog](https://in.fintaxblog.com/budget-2026-27-customs-duty-on-gold-and-silver-imports-remains-unchanged/); contradicted by [Aditya Birla Capital](https://www.adityabirlacapital.com/abc-of-money/budget-2026-impact-on-jewellery-prices)

### Inferences
- The duty hike raised the landed cost by a factor of 1.15/1.06 = 1.085, about +8.5%. For someone who held from before May, this partly offset the global fall. Combined with the 2026 rupee depreciation (about 3%), Indian prices were cushioned by about 12 points against dollar prices year to date. This is my calculation and ignores the domestic discount.
- Risk for a video point: if the duty is cut back to 6% later (for example in Budget 2027), domestic prices would fall about 7.8% (1.06/1.15 − 1) with no change in world prices. This is a scenario, not a forecast.

## 4. Domestic premium/discount vs the international price (Reuters dealer quotes)

### Takeaway
Indian dealers swung between discounts and premiums as prices whipsawed. The discount was up to $61/oz in the run to the Jan 2026 peak, with brief premiums of up to $15 in early Jan. It hit a record of more than $200/oz (up to $207) on the day of the May 2026 duty hike, as dealers dumped duty-paid stock. September 2026 averaged a $66 discount. It narrowed to $5 by 12 Nov, and some reports show a premium of up to $7 by October/November as festive buying returned on the price fall.

### Cited Findings
- Early Jan 2026: India and China flipped to premiums as prices retreated from record highs. Indian dealers charged up to $15/oz over official domestic prices, against a discount of up to $61 the prior week (Reuters) — [Business Standard/Reuters, 2 Jan 2026](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/commodities/india-china-flip-to-gold-premiums-as-prices-retreat-from-record-highs-126010200403_1.html)
- Early Apr 2026: first premiums in two months. Discounts of up to $8 and premiums of up to $2/oz, against discounts of up to $61 the week before (Reuters) — [Kitco/Reuters, 2 Apr 2026](https://www.kitco.com/news/off-the-wire/2026-04-02/indian-dealers-charge-first-gold-premiums-two-months)
- 13 May 2026 (duty hike day): discounts reached a record of more than $200/oz (up to $207), from about $17 the day before (Reuters) — [Kitco/Reuters, 13 May 2026](https://www.kitco.com/news/off-the-wire/2026-05-13/gold-discounts-india-breach-200ounce-record-amid-profit-taking)
- June 2026: discounts on legal gold above $100/oz made refining dore uneconomical, since refiners' margin is about 0.65% (CGR Metalloys, via Reuters) — [Kitco/Reuters, 9 Jun 2026](https://www.kitco.com/news/off-the-wire/2026-06-09/indias-gold-tariff-hike-fuels-smuggling-revival-squeezes-banks-and-0)
- WGC: domestic discounts narrowed from an average of $66/oz in Sept 2026 to $5/oz by 12 Nov, as seasonal demand improved. Industry feedback points to old-gold supply and unofficial inflows keeping discounts persistent — [WGC India market update, Nov 2026](https://www.gold.org/goldhub/gold-focus/2026/11/india-gold-market-update-gold-demand-gets-festive-boost)
- Oct 2026 (non-Reuters): dealers moved from a discount of up to $6 to a premium of up to $7/oz, the first premium in more than three months — [Discovery Alert](https://discoveryalert.com/news/india-physical-gold-premium-asia-october-2026/)
- Earlier context: in March 2025, discounts hit an 8-month high as record prices dampened demand — [Business Standard/Reuters, 14 Mar 2025](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/commodities/gold-discounts-in-india-hit-8-month-high-as-record-prices-dampen-demand-125031400267_1.html)

### Inferences
- The pattern is that discounts widen when prices spike or right after a duty hike, and premiums return when prices fall and festive or wedding buyers come back. The Nov 2026 move from a $66 discount to about flat or premium is consistent with the price fall bringing out bargain buyers.
- A persistent discount after the 15% duty also reflects smuggled and recycled supply undercutting duty-paid metal (Section 5).

## 5. Demand: Dhanteras/Diwali 2025 and 2026, weddings, imports (value and tonnes), WGC quarterly data

### Takeaway
Volumes are in a multi-year slump: WGC's 2026 India forecast is 625–675 t, possibly a six-year low. Spending in rupees, though, hit records, because prices did the work. Q2 2026 was 130.9 t (-6% year on year) and Q3 162.5 t (-29%). Dhanteras 2026 (6 Nov) value was up about 15–20% but volume was roughly flat. Official imports crashed after the duty hike (Oct 2026 $5.51 bn, -63% year on year), but smuggling revived. The November price fall is bringing wedding-season buyers back.

## 10. Gold loans: growth, RBI rules, and what a price fall means for borrowers

### Takeaway
Gold loans boomed: about ₹3.74 trillion outstanding in Sept 2026, up about 60–66% year on year, faster than gold's roughly 27% price rise. New RBI directions effective 1 Apr 2026 set LTV caps of 85%, 80% and 75% by loan size and capped bullet loans at 12 months. With prices down about 17% from end-September, loans made near the top at high LTV can breach limits. Reports say RBI is monitoring portfolios where LTV went above 100%. Muthoot's Stage 2 book jumped and the NBFC stocks fell about 5%.

### Cited Findings
- Outstanding gold loans were about ₹3.74 trillion in Sept 2026, up 66.1% year on year (Moneycontrol, via secondary). CRIF's figure is +60.6%, while gold rose about 27% (₹1.17 lakh to ₹1.50 lakh per 10 g) over the same period — [India Fintech Substack](https://indiafintech.substack.com/p/how-india-lends-q2-fy27-the-monthly); [Lapaas Voice](https://lapaasvoice.com/rbi-steps-up-monitoring-of-gold-loan-portfolios-as-falling-prices-push-ltv-ratios-above-100)
- The RBI Lending Against Gold and Silver Collateral Directions, 2025 were notified 6 Jun 2025, updated 29 Sept 2025 and took effect 1 Apr 2026. LTV caps are 85% for loans up to ₹2.5 lakh, 80% for ₹2.5–5 lakh and 75% above ₹5 lakh. Bullet loans are capped at 12 months, with a ₹5,000/day penalty for late return of pledged gold — [The Money Decoded](https://themoneydecoded.com/blog/rbi-gold-loan-rules-2026); [Multibagg](https://www.multibagg.ai/market-pulse/articles/rbi-gold-loan-rules-2026-cmqz8u5z8o0ato20j7ajy0lwr); [Upstox](https://upstox.com/learning-center/personal-finance/rbi-gold-loan-rules-2026/article-1545/)
- Lapaas Voice (citing Moneycontrol) says RBI stepped up monitoring because falling prices pushed some LTV ratios above 100% — [Lapaas Voice](https://lapaasvoice.com/rbi-steps-up-monitoring-of-gold-loan-portfolios-as-falling-prices-push-ltv-ratios-above-100)
- Muthoot's Stage 2 gold loans were about ₹23,467 crore, against ₹1,876 crore a quarter earlier (or ₹534 crore a year earlier; sources differ), with an LTV of 72% on Stage 2 and 53% on Stage 3. Manappuram's Stage 2 share rose from about 0.5% to about 5% — [India Fintech Substack](https://indiafintech.substack.com/p/how-india-lends-q2-fy27-the-monthly)
- Muthoot and Manappuram shares fell about 5%. ICICI Securities linked the pressure to the April 2026 rules' tighter LTV and repayment requirements. Muthoot hit a 52-week low of ₹2,305 — [Business Standard, 18 Nov 2026](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/gold-financiers-under-pressure-muthoot-manappuram-fall-5-here-s-why-126111800267_1.html)
- HDFC Securities published a gold loan NBFC thematic in Jul 2026 — [HDFC Sec PDF](https://www.hdfcsec.com/hsl.docs/Gold%20Loans%20-%20Sector%20Thematic%20-%20HSIE-202607071600249489907.pdf)

### Inferences
- Mechanically, a loan at 85% LTV becomes about 100% LTV after a roughly 15% fall in the gold price (0.85/0.85 → 0.85/0.723). With domestic gold about 17% below end-Sept, borrowers who took maximum-LTV loans in Sept–Oct 2026 may face top-up or part-payment demands, or auction risk. This is a key "what it means for ordinary Indians" point.
- The Stage 2 jump at Muthoot may reflect the new 12-month bullet-repayment rules as much as the price fall. The sources don't separate the two.

## 11. Other government/RBI measures (Gold Monetisation Scheme, etc.)

### Takeaway
The government shut the medium- and long-term parts of the Gold Monetisation Scheme from 26 March 2025 and stopped SGB issues. Both removed sovereign gold-price liabilities as prices soared. In 2026 the policy mix turned actively anti-gold-import: the PM's appeal (10 May), the 15% duty (13 May), silver import licensing (May–June), and the October MF FoF halts. A 6,500 t silver import approval on 23 Oct loosened silver supply.

### Cited Findings
- GMS medium- and long-term government deposits were discontinued from 26 Mar 2025. Banks may still offer short-term (1–3 year) bank deposits at their discretion, and existing deposits run to maturity — [Business Standard, 25 Mar 2025](https://www.business-standard.com/economy/news/govt-to-discontinue-medium-long-term-gold-monetisation-scheme-from-march-125032501337_1.html); [RBI notification](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=12801&Mode=0); [News On Air, 26 Mar 2025](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/mltgd-components-of-gold-monetisation-scheme-to-be-discontinued-from-today); [Mining.com](https://www.mining.com/web/india-discontinues-parts-of-gold-deposit-scheme-amid-rising-prices/)
- For the 2026 measures (PM appeal, duty hike, silver licensing, MF FoF halt, 6,500 t silver import approval), see Sections 3, 5 and 6 for sources.

### Inferences
- On the India-side "why did it fall" story: in 2026 the state worked to cut gold demand to save dollars. This was a dampener during the rise and is now claimed (by the government) as a cause of the fall. Independent evidence shows Indian demand fell in volume, but world prices are driven mainly by the dollar, rates and central banks (other researchers' scope).
