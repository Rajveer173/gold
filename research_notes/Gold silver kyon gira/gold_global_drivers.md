## Q0. Price path: how big was the fall, and when did it happen?

### Takeaway
Gold peaked on 29 Jan 2026 (spot about $5,595 intraday; futures about $5,608-5,626). By 20 Nov 2026 it had fallen to about $3,520-3,537, roughly 35-37% below the peak and a 14-month low. The fall came in four waves: the Warsh-nomination crash (30 Jan), the Iran-war/March liquidation, a slow bleed through the Fed's turn to rate hikes (Jun-Oct), and a forced-selling crash on 17-20 Nov.

## Q1. Monetary policy: what did the Fed do, who is chair, and how did real yields and the dollar move?

### Takeaway
This is the dominant driver. The Fed went from cutting in 2025 to hiking in 2026. It held at 3.50-3.75% in June, hiked 25bp to 3.75-4.00% on 16 Sep (its first hike in three years), and by 20 Nov markets priced a December hike at about 97%. The cause was oil-shock and AI-capex inflation (October CPI +0.4% m/m, about 4% y/y). Kevin Warsh's nomination as chair (30 Jan) set off the first crash. The 10-yr TIPS real yield rose from 1.72% (27 Feb) to a 2.95% peak (5 Oct), and a 19 Nov auction cleared at 2.903%, the highest since 2002. The dollar index reached a roughly 19-20-month high near 103.7-103.8.

- A real yield near 2.9% is the key teaching number. An investor can lock in about 2.9% above inflation in a risk-free US bond, so the opportunity cost of holding zero-yield gold is the highest in about 24 years.
- The dollar effect is real but secondary. The DXY rise from about 100.5 (June) to about 103.7 (Nov) is about +3%, small next to gold's -35%. WGC also called the dollar's March rise "modest".

## Q2. Geopolitics and risk: did de-escalation remove the safe-haven bid?

### Takeaway
De-escalation was a clear trigger in the 2025 corrections: the US-China trade truce in Oct 2025 and Russia-Ukraine peace-talk optimism on 29 Dec 2025. In 2026 the story flips. The US-Israeli war with Iran hurt gold rather than helping it, because it pushed oil and inflation up and so pushed rate hikes up. Ceasefire news had mixed effects; sometimes gold rose on lower inflation fears. Commentators call the 2026 slide "a bet on the Fed, not on peace".

- A simple narrative for Indian viewers: "America's interest rates went up, so gold's shine faded."
