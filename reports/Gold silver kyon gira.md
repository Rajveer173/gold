# Rate Hikes and Margin Calls Sank Bullion

Gold and silver fell for two reasons. The US Federal Reserve swung from cutting interest rates to raising them, and a market full of borrowed money was forced to sell into that swing. Gold fell about **37%**, from an intraday record near **$5,590 (28–29 Jan 2026)** to **$3,513.54 on 20 Nov 2026**. Silver fell about **66%**, from **$121.64** to **$41.12**. The fall came in six legs. The first was the 30 Jan crash after Kevin Warsh was named as the next Fed chair and exchanges raised margins: gold fell 9.5% and silver futures 31%, their worst days since 1983 and 1980. Then came a March sell-off during the Iran war, a mid-year grind, and the Fed's first hike since 2023 on 16 Sep. The last leg was a November rout: markets priced a December hike at about 97%, US real yields hit their highest since 2002, and an AI-stock crash forced investors to sell gold to meet margin calls. The Iran war hurt gold instead of helping it, because higher oil prices pushed up inflation and brought rate hikes. Silver fell about twice as hard as gold. Solar-panel makers cut their silver use, the premium from the 2025 London squeeze and US tariff fears disappeared, and speculators left. Central banks kept buying and did not cause the fall. In rupees, Indians lost less. MCX gold is about **32% below its ₹1,80,501 peak**, and IBJA 24K gold is down only about **7% for 2026**, against about 16% in dollars. The reason is the **13 May hike in import duty from 6% to 15%**, which lifted domestic prices by about 8.5%. That cushion would reverse by about 7.8% if the duty were cut back. The losses fall mainly on three groups: people who bought in 2026 (a record ₹24,040 crore went into gold ETFs in the peak month), leveraged MCX traders, and borrowers with gold loans at the maximum loan-to-value (LTV) ratio. Buyers from before 2025 and holders of 2020–21 Sovereign Gold Bonds are still well in profit. The evidence supports a calm, rules-based response: keep a target allocation (gold about 5–10%, silver a small tactical slice), add only in steps through regulated ETFs or fund-of-funds (FoFs), avoid leverage, and watch the 9 Dec Fed decision, real yields, the dollar, the rupee and the duty decision in the 1 Feb 2027 Budget.

### Cause 1: the Fed went from cutting to hiking, and real yields hit a 24-year high

Timing is the strongest evidence. The 30 Jan crash followed news that President Trump would nominate Kevin Warsh as Fed chair. Markets read him as hawkish and pro-dollar, which undercut the debasement story ([Finviz](https://finviz.com/news/293188/trumps-fed-choice-kevin-warsh-sparks-dollar-surge-metals-crash)). Warsh was sworn in on 22 May after a 54–45 Senate vote **[1 source]** ([Advantage Gold](https://www.advantagegold.com/blog/kevin-warsh-fed-chair-confirmed-gold/)). His first meeting, on 16–17 Jun, was a "hawkish hold" at 3.50–3.75%: 9 of 18 officials pencilled in a 2026 hike, and the dollar index rose 0.9% to 100.5 ([Yahoo/AFP](https://uk.finance.yahoo.com/news/us-federal-kicks-off-first-141534974.html)). On **16 Sep the Fed raised rates 25 basis points (bp) to 3.75–4.00%**, its first hike since 2023, by a 12–0 vote. Sixteen of 18 officials expected more hikes. The reported reasons were tariffs, the energy shock after the US-Israeli war with Iran began, and AI-related capital spending ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/16/gold-gains-with-fed-rate-decision-in-spotlight.html); [Kitco](https://www.kitco.com/news/article/2026-09-16/gold-price-drops-4310oz-fed-votes-12-0-favor-25-bps-rate-hike-16-18)). The Fed held on 28 Oct, with two dissents in favour of a hike ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/28/fed-meeting-today-live-updates.html)).

November then brought three blows in a row. October CPI, released on 10 Nov, rose 0.4% month on month against 0.3% expected; core CPI rose 0.3% against 0.2%; headline inflation was about 4% year on year ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/cpi_11102026.htm); [Kiplinger](https://www.kiplinger.com/investing/economy/october-2026-cpi-report-what-to-expect)). The minutes released on 18 Nov said "almost all participants" saw further hikes as likely appropriate ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/18/fed-minutes-october-2026.html)). The same day, Governor Christopher Waller said a 50bp December hike was "probably necessary" ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/18/federal-reserve-governor-christopher-waller.html)). Market odds of a hike at the 8–9 Dec meeting went from about 75% after the October meeting to **about 97%** by 20 Nov ([JPMorgan](https://www.jpmorgan.com/insights/global-research/economy/fed-rate-cuts); [investinglive](https://investinglive.com/commodities/gold-sinks-9-in-a-week-as-margin-calls-and-fed-hike-bets-override-its-haven-role/)).

The channel is the real yield, the return on an inflation-protected US government bond. The 10-year TIPS yield rose from **1.72% on 27 Feb to 2.95% on 5 Oct** ([Macrotrends](https://www.macrotrends.net/3010/10-year-tips-yield)). A 19 Nov auction cleared at **2.903%, the highest for that maturity since July 2002** ([TIPSWatch](https://tipswatch.com/2026/11/19/10-year-tips-auction-gets-real-yield-of-2-903-highest-in-24-years/)). Investors can now lock in almost 3% above inflation with no risk, so holding gold, which pays nothing, is costlier than at any time in 24 years. This is the core teaching point for viewers. The banks blamed the same channel when they cut their forecasts. JPMorgan's 3 Jul note pointed to "a sharp re-emergence of gold's sensitivity to real interest rates" *[view]* ([TheStreet](https://www.thestreet.com/investing/jpmorgan-tweaks-gold-price-target-as-fed-risks-return)). Goldman cut twice in November, citing a "hawkish Fed shift" *[view]* ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/commodities/articles/goldman-lowers-end-gold-price-081423381.html)).

### Cause 2: leverage turned each Fed shock into a crash

Borrowed money explains why the falls were so violent. CME (the US futures exchange) kept raising margins as the rally peaked and broke. At the end of December 2025 it raised silver margins 13.6%, to $25,000 per contract ([AP/Washington Times](https://m.washingtontimes.com/news/2025/dec/29/surging-silver-gold-slide-cme-raises-margin-requirements/)). On 28 Jan 2026, the day before the peak, it lifted them again from 9% to 11% of contract value ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-01-28/cme-raises-silver-margins-as-prices-smash-records-in-wild-rally)). One account counts **five margin hikes in nine days** around the crash, ending with a post-crash increase effective 2 Feb that lifted maintenance margins by 33% for gold and 36% for silver ([stockalarm.io](https://pro.stockalarm.io/blog/silver-crash-cme-margin-increase-2026)). Each hike forced leveraged traders to post more cash or sell, and many sold. India followed. MCX set margins at 20% for gold and 25% for silver from 1 Feb and added extra margins on 5–6 Feb ([JM Financial](https://www.jmfinancialservices.in/market-news-and-insights/1669898)). ICICI Securities estimated that effective silver margins rose from about 15% to **72%**, and gold from about 10% to 30%, until the extra margins were withdrawn on 19 Feb ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/markets/stocks/story/mcx-shares-additional-margin-on-gold-silver-futures-withdrawn-what-icici-sec-says-516865-2026-02-19)).

In March the WGC said investors were "selling what they could rather than what they wanted to be rid of". That describes a sale to raise cash ([Kitco/WGC](https://www.kitco.com/news/article/2026-04-08/dissecting-what-really-happened-and-what-didnt-during-golds-iran-war)). Gold ETFs lost a **record $12bn** that month ([BullionVault](https://www.bullionvault.com/gold-news/gold-price-news/gold-central-banks-etfs-033120261)).

November repeated the pattern. As the AI-stock rout deepened, investors sold gold to meet margin calls ([investinglive](https://investinglive.com/commodities/gold-sinks-9-in-a-week-as-margin-calls-and-fed-hike-bets-override-its-haven-role/)). ING noted that gold "kept falling ... even as Treasury yields eased and the dollar's momentum cooled, a sign that liquidation, not rates alone, was doing the damage" *[view]* ([investinglive](https://investinglive.com/commodities/gold-sinks-9-in-a-week-as-margin-calls-and-fed-hike-bets-override-its-haven-role/)). The 20 Nov data support ING: the 10-year real yield fell 11bp to 2.77% while gold fell 3–4% ([Businessquant](https://businessquant.com/economic-data/ustips10y)). Between 30 Oct and 20 Nov, gold and the S&P 500 each fell 8.8% ([BullionVault](https://www.bullionvault.com/gold-news/gold-price-news/gold-silver-crash-rally-112320261)). That is how a "safe haven" behaves when it is the most liquid, most profitable asset in a losing portfolio. CME's latest margin increase came *after* the slide, so it was a reaction, not a trigger: gold from 5% to 6% and silver from 8% to 9%, reported on 24 Nov **[1 source]** ([Invezz](https://invezz.com/sg/news/2026/11/24/gold-prices-fall-as-cme-raises-margins-by-20percent-is-another-crash-coming/)).

The washout may not be complete. On 17 Nov, speculative fund bets on higher gold prices on COMEX (managed-money net longs) stood at 102,942 contracts. That is 29% below the 25 Aug peak but still near the five-year median ([Businessquant](https://businessquant.com/economic-data/cotgoldmm)). Global gold ETF holdings were at a **record 4,275t at end-October**: Europe drove $2.7bn of inflows, and North America was the only region selling, with -$512m ([WGC](https://www.gold.org/goldhub/research/gold-etfs-holdings-and-flows/2026/11)). Goldman warned that ETF holders who had not sold could give up and push spot gold below $3,890 *[view]* ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/commodities/articles/goldman-lowers-end-gold-price-081423381.html)). Spot is already below that level. WGC's November ETF data, due in early December, will show whether that selling happened.

### Cause 3: the Iran war hurt gold because oil meant rate hikes

In 2026 war did not lift gold. On 8 Apr Forbes noted that gold and silver had "generally declined throughout the Iran war", moving opposite to oil ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/conormurray/2026/04/08/gold-and-silver-hit-3-week-highs-after-us-iran-ceasefire-agreement/)). March was the war's first full month; the notes could not confirm the exact start date. Gold fell 12% that month, because rising oil ended hopes of rate cuts ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/03/31/gold-on-track-for-worst-month-since-2008-as-iran-war-drags-on.html)). When the US and Iran agreed a two-week ceasefire on 8 Apr, gold rose almost 2% to $4,790, because fears of energy-driven inflation eased ([Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/commodity/gold/news/540173)). The war also created forced sellers among central banks. Official holders were net sellers of 30t in March, led by Turkey and Russia ([Modern Diplomacy](https://moderndiplomacy.eu/2026/11/22/gold-sell-off-2026-fed-rate-hikes-iran-war-central-banks/)). BullionVault reported a monthly record for central-bank gold selling by value ([BullionVault](https://www.bullionvault.com/gold-news/market-fundamentals/central-bank-gold-040920262)). Modern Diplomacy's 22 Nov summary is a good one-liner for the video: "Gold's slide is a bet on the Fed, not on peace. Peace may be what rescues it" *[view]*. In 2025 the logic ran the other way. Easing tensions (the US-China truce in October, optimism over Ukraine talks on 29 Dec) triggered falls of 4.5–6% inside a bull market ([Kitco](https://www.kitco.com/opinion/2025-10-27/gold-retreats-below-4000-amid-easing-trade-tensions); [AP/Washington Times](https://m.washingtontimes.com/news/2025/dec/29/surging-silver-gold-slide-cme-raises-margin-requirements/)).

### Causes 4 and 5: a firmer dollar and buyers who left before the crash

The dollar mattered, but only at the edges. The dollar index rose from about 100.5 in June to a 13 Nov close of 103.41, the highest in a year ([KuCoin](https://www.kucoin.com/news/insight/BTC/6af9bfcc40903b0007ae295f)), and about 103.65 around 18 Nov, the highest since April 2025 ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/18/gold-slides-to-over-one-year-low-on-firm-dollar-us-rate-outlook.html)). That is roughly +3%, against gold's -35% *(calc.)*. The WGC called the dollar's rise in March "modest" ([Kitco/WGC](https://www.kitco.com/news/article/2026-04-08/dissecting-what-really-happened-and-what-didnt-during-golds-iran-war)). On 24 Nov gold fell even as the dollar eased, because bets on rate hikes dominated ([FXStreet](https://www.fxstreet.com/news/gold-slides-despite-weaker-us-dollar-as-fed-hike-bets-dominate-202611241748)).

Physical buying weakened *before* the crash, so it removed a cushion rather than causing the fall. In WGC's Q3 2026 data, global jewellery demand fell 17% to 313t, and bars and coins fell 19% to 268t. Total demand slipped only 1%, to 1,369t, because ETFs (+211t) and central banks (321t) took up the slack ([WGC](https://www.gold.org/goldhub/research/gold-demand-trends/gold-demand-trends-q3-2026)). China's jewellery demand fell 32% in Q1, to 85t, after Beijing ended a VAT exemption on 1 Nov 2025. India's Q1 jewellery demand fell 19%, to 66.1t ([WGC](https://www.gold.org/goldhub/research/gold-demand-trends/gold-demand-trends-q1-2026/jewellery)).

### What did not cause it: central banks, and probably not India's buying pause

Central banks bought **321t in Q3 2026, up 42% year on year**, well above the five-year quarterly average of 227t ([WGC](https://www.gold.org/goldhub/research/gold-demand-trends/gold-demand-trends-q3-2026/central-banks)). China's central bank added 25.8t in October. That was its 24th straight month of buying and its largest addition since September 2023 ([China Daily HK](https://www.chinadailyhk.com/article/642391)). Goldman expects about 60t a month through 2026–27, against a pre-2022 average of 17t, and calls this the main support for the price *[view]* ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/commodities/articles/goldman-lowers-end-gold-price-081423381.html)). The RBI neither bought nor sold. Its holdings stayed at 880.52t, and it denied a June report that it had sold $12bn of gold to defend the rupee ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/economy/news/rbi-denies-reports-of-selling-12-bn-worth-of-gold-reserves-to-rescue-rupee-126060300621_1.html)).

In November, PM Modi said that Indians who followed his 10 May appeal to pause gold buying had "saved money" ([The Week](https://www.theweek.in/news/india/2026/11/21/pm-modi-advice-gold-price-crash.html)). Commerce Minister Piyush Goyal said "Indians influenced global gold prices" ([ANI](https://aninews.in/news/national/politics/indians-influenced-global-gold-prices-union-minister-piyush-goyal-hails-pm-modis-appeal-to-reduce-gold-purchases20261122151843)). Treat both as government claims *[view]*. India's official imports did collapse: October imports fell 62.6% year on year ([ANI via inkl](https://www.inkl.com/news/indias-gold-imports-plunge-62-57-to-5-51-billion-in-october-as-prices-fall-below-4-000-oz)). But the big falls line up with Fed dates, not Indian data, and global demand barely moved. Indian restraint probably removed some support. The evidence does not show that it moved the world price.

### Where analysts disagree

Four live disagreements are worth showing honestly, and all of them are views rather than settled facts. The first is **what drove November**: ING and Peter Schiff emphasise liquidation and margin calls, while Goldman, JPMorgan and Modern Diplomacy emphasise the Fed and real rates ([Discovery Alert](https://discoveryalert.com/analysis/gold-silver-sell-off-evidence-november-2026/)). The second is **what drove October**. The WGC's own model says falling yields *supported* gold that month, so it blames "asset rotation, de-grossing and Fed rate-path repricing" instead ([WGC](https://www.gold.org/goldhub/research/gold-market-commentary-october-2026)). The third is **whether the bottom is close**. UBS calls the sell-off "positioning-led, not structural" ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/commodities/articles/gold-rebounds-traders-pare-bets-002803539.html)), and the WGC says gold "may be nearing an interim bottom", with two of its three conditions met ([newsd.in](https://newsd.in/gold-market-outlook-wgc-sees-possible-bottom-after-sharp-2026-price-correction/)). On the other side, Goldman flags the risk of ETF holders giving up, and Saxo's Ole Hansen warns that the "brutal sell-off may not be over" ([TheStreet](https://thestreet.com/investing/ole-hansen-gold-sell-off)). The fourth is **what caused silver's 30 Jan crash**: some blame Warsh and the dollar, others CME margin mechanics ([stockalarm.io](https://pro.stockalarm.io/blog/silver-crash-cme-margin-increase-2026)). No source in the notes offers a formal model that settles any of these.

## Silver fell twice as far because its shortage story collapsed

Silver's -66%, against gold's -37%, reflects three supports that only silver had. All three collapsed on top of the shared rate shock. From 30 Oct to 20 Nov silver fell 15.4%, **exactly in line with copper**, while gold fell 8.8%. In this leg silver traded like an industrial metal, not like money ([BullionVault](https://www.bullionvault.com/gold-news/gold-price-news/gold-silver-crash-rally-112320261)).

**The shortage premium disappeared.** In October 2025 London ran short of freely available silver. One-month lease rates (the cost of borrowing physical silver) hit about 39%, against a normal level below 1% ([FinancialContent](https://markets.financialcontent.com/stocks/article/marketminute-2025-10-10-silvers-historic-squeeze-deepens-london-market-in-chaos-as-prices-soar-past-50)). About 225 Moz had moved from London to New York vaults between December 2024 and October 2025 on fears of US tariffs ([Kitco](https://www.kitco.com/opinion/2026-04-23/londons-silver-running-out-room)). Indian festive buying added pressure; one headline read "sold out in India, panic in London" ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/commodities/sold-out-in-india-panic-in-london-what-triggered-the-silver-market-chaos-125101900064_1.html)). By mid-Q1 2026 lease rates were back to about 2–3% ([Kitco](https://www.kitco.com/opinion/2026-04-23/londons-silver-running-out-room)). By end-September London vaults held **932 Moz, the most in nearly four years**, and available stock was close to double its October 2025 low ([Kitco](https://www.kitco.com/news/article/2026-10-29/silver-could-see-further-losses-against-gold-physical-market-weakens-metals)). The "silver is running out" story no longer fit the facts when the autumn sell-off began.

**The tariff premium came out.** Silver was added to the US Critical Minerals List on 7 Nov 2025, which made it eligible for Section 232 tariffs ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/11/07/2025-19813/final-2025-list-of-critical-minerals)). On 14–15 Jan 2026 Trump held off on tariffs, choosing bilateral deals and a possible price floor instead. Silver fell more than 7% from above $93 in one day ([ING](https://think.ing.com/articles/trump-puts-silver-tariffs-on-hold); [Kitco](https://www.kitco.com/news/article/2026-01-15/silver-price-falling-trump-will-not-impose-critical-minerals-tariffs-now)). The rally still ran for two more weeks. The 180-day review window ended around 13 Jul, and no public outcome was found **[status unconfirmed]** ([Crux Investor](https://www.cruxinvestor.com/posts/section-232-tariff-deadline-a-600-000-tonne-copper-deficit-push-capital-toward-deliverable-projects)).

**High prices destroyed demand.** In April, the Silver Institute's World Silver Survey forecast a 2026 supply deficit of 46.3 Moz ([Kitco](https://www.kitco.com/news/article/2026-04-15/silver-market-faces-another-deficit-2026-volatility-and-investment-demand)). Its 12 Nov interim review cut that to **35 Moz, the narrowest in six years**. Total demand is down 5%, to 1.08bn oz, and industrial demand is down 3%, to 640 Moz ([Silver Institute](https://silverinstitute.org/silver-market-set-for-sixth-consecutive-annual-deficit-in-2026/)). Solar is the swing factor. Silver use in solar panels was forecast to fall about 19% in 2026, to roughly 151 Moz, the largest drop on record for silver's biggest industrial use ([IndexBox](https://www.indexbox.io/blog/silver-market-faces-sixth-consecutive-deficit-despite-solar-industry-cutting-usage/)). Manufacturers are using less silver per panel and switching to copper; Metals Focus's Philip Newman calls it "a very aggressive move" *[view]* ([Northern Miner](https://www.northernminer.com/news/solar-thrifting-not-enough-to-curb-demand/1003891836/)). A 35 Moz gap is only about 3% of demand *(calc.)*, and existing stockpiles are covering it. Industrial demand outside solar still grew 4.5%, to 492 Moz ([Silver Institute](https://silverinstitute.org/silver-market-set-for-sixth-consecutive-annual-deficit-in-2026/)). So the electrification and AI case for silver has been dented, not destroyed. The outlook for 2027 is now openly disputed. Metals Focus expects a **surplus in 2027** ([Kitco](https://www.kitco.com/news/article/2026-10-29/silver-could-see-further-losses-against-gold-physical-market-weakens-metals)), while Bank of America expects a deficit *[both views]* ([Finbold](https://finbold.com/banking-giant-sets-silver-price-for-2027/)).

**Positioning amplified every move.** On 30 Jan, SLV (the largest silver ETF) lost 31% and leveraged silver ETFs lost more than 60% ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/01/30/silver-gold-fall-price-usd-dollar-fed-warsh-chair-trump-metals.html)). CME raised silver margins again on 6 Feb, from 15% to 18% ([GoldSilver.com](https://goldsilver.com/industry-news/article/what-are-margin-requirements-why-cmes-hike-triggered-a-silver-crash/)). Global silver ETF holdings fell by 55 Moz (-4%) in 2026, to 1.25bn oz by 3 Nov ([Silver Institute](https://silverinstitute.org/silver-market-set-for-sixth-consecutive-annual-deficit-in-2026/)). Silver coin sales at leading mints fell more than 80% between Q1 and Q2, to the lowest level since Metals Focus began its survey in 2010 ([Kitco](https://www.kitco.com/news/article/2026-10-29/silver-could-see-further-losses-against-gold-physical-market-weakens-metals)). Speculative funds' net bets on higher silver prices on COMEX shrank to just **about 1,280 contracts on 17 Nov** ([Business Quant](https://businessquant.com/economic-data/cotsilvermm)), so speculators have almost entirely left. Contrarians may read that as a sign of a bottom. BullionVault says silver's 39% loss in the 13 weeks to 24 Nov (to $40.76) was its steepest since the 2008 Lehman crash **[1 source]** ([BullionVault](https://www.bullionvault.com/gold-news/gold-price-news/gold-silver-fed-mistake-112420261)).

History shows silver slumps can last years. Silver's 2011 peak of about $49.51 fell 72%, to $13.71, by December 2015 ([Kitco](https://www.kitco.com/opinion/2025-11-05/silver-high-where-was-it-1980-how-did-it-develop-2011-and-bear-market-looming)). After the 1980 Hunt-brothers peak, silver fell about 75% **[conflict: Kitco says about 90%]** and did not regain its high until 2011 ([PeerMetals](https://peermetals.com/blog/silver-crash-history)). "Silver Thursday" (27 Mar 1980) is put at -33% in a day by Kitco and about -50% by PeerMetals **[conflict]**. In both 1980 and 2026, exchanges raising margins turned a borrowed-money rally into a crash ([APMEX](https://learn.apmex.com/learning-guide/history/silver-thursday-the-hunt-brothers-scheme/)).

India added its own twist to silver. From about 16 May, silver imports were moved from "Free" to "Restricted". May imports fell about 94%, to 33t ([Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/business/indias-silver-imports-plummet-87-as-supply-is-hampered-by-import-restrictions/)) **[conflict: another count gives 46.8t against 534.3t ([FXStreet](https://www.fxstreet.com/analysis/why-indias-silver-imports-fell-91-in-a-month-202608111524))]**. By July, domestic silver premiums were at a six-month high ([Kitco/Reuters](https://www.kitco.com/news/off-the-wire/2026-07-08/india-silver-import-curbs-create-shortages-push-premiums-six-month)). On 23 Oct the government approved 6,500t of imports over six months, removing that scarcity premium. Indian silver ETFs fell about 4% that day **[effectively 1 source]** ([Techyorker](https://techyorker.com/why-indian-silver-etfs-fell-up-to-4-after-the-6500-ton-import-approval-on-october-23-2026/)). MCX silver then slid from ₹1,79,132/kg at end-October to ₹1,48,900 by 21 Nov ([The Open Press](https://theopenpress.in/article/gold-silver-price-crash-silver-271-lakh-gold-72000-fall)). Indian analysts cite a stronger dollar, solar makers using less silver, profit-taking and bets on Fed hikes *[views]* ([TradingView/Mint](https://www.tradingview.com/news/moodys:a1cd83def5cea:0-why-silver-rates-crashed-from-record-high-of-rs-4-lakh-per-kg-price-to-below-rs-1-75-lakh-experts-explain-3-key-reasons/); [Zee Business](https://www.zeebiz.com/markets/commodities/news-silver-prices-crash-over-60-from-january-peak-is-more-downside-ahead-experts-weigh-in-405723)).

### The duty, not the rupee, explains most of India's smaller loss

Measured from the 29 Jan peak, MCX gold fell about 32.5% while spot gold fell 37.1% *(calc.)*. The rupee explains little of that gap: it was just above 92 per dollar on 29 Jan and 92.62 on 20 Nov. The duty explains most of it. On **13 May 2026 India raised the import duty on gold and silver from 6% to 15%**: basic customs duty went from 5% to 10%, and the AIDC (an agriculture cess) from 1% to 5%, with 3% IGST on top ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/05/13/india-hikes-bullion-import-duties-to-arrest-rupee-slide.html); [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/custom-duty/customs-duty-gold-silver-raised-15-percent-effective-13-may-2026.html)). The hike came three days after PM Modi asked Indians to stop buying gold for a year to protect foreign-exchange reserves ([FXStreet](https://www.fxstreet.com/news/modi-to-india-dont-buy-gold-for-a-year-202605121251)). The hike raises the landed cost by 1.15/1.06, about **+8.5%** *(calc.)*, and MCX gold jumped 7.2% that day ([ICICI Direct](https://www.icicidirect.com/research/equity/finace/gold-and-silver-import-duty-raised-from-6-to-15-what-it-means-for-gold-and-silver-prices-and-jewellery-stocks); [The Week](https://www.theweek.in/news/biz-tech/2026/05/13/india-gold-import-duty-hike.html)). In MCX terms, the price in rupees per 10g for each dollar per ounce rose from about 32.3 at the peak to about 34.6 on 20 Nov, roughly +7% *(calc.)*. For 2026 so far, the duty plus a rupee about 3% weaker than its 2025 close of 89.88 ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/finance/news/rupee-enters-2026-on-back-foot-after-worst-annual-drop-in-3-years-125123100230_1.html)) explains why IBJA gold is down only about 7%, against about 16% in dollars *(calc.)*. BusinessToday put it the same way on 9 Nov: "Gold falls 21% globally but only 15% in India" ([BusinessToday](https://www.businesstoday.in/latest/economy/story/gold-falls-21-globally-but-only-15-in-india-how-the-rupee-protected-investors-566568-2026-11-09)).

The cushion comes from policy, so policy can take it away. The July 2024 Budget cut the duty from 15% to 6% ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/business/banking/india-budget-2024-custom-duties-on-gold-silver-slashed-down-to-6-from-15-1.1721716047012)). A similar cut now would knock about **7.8%** off domestic prices overnight, with no change in world prices *(calc.)*. The 1 Feb 2026 Budget left the duty at 6% ([NewsBytes](https://www.newsbytesapp.com/news/business/union-budget-2026-no-change-in-gold-silver-import-duties/story)) **[conflict: Aditya Birla Capital claims a cut to 5% ([ABC](https://www.adityabirlacapital.com/abc-of-money/budget-2026-impact-on-jewellery-prices)); the majority say unchanged]**. The notes found no sign of a planned rollback before Budget 2027. The hike also revived smuggling. The grey-market margin roughly doubled from about 9% to 18%, and dealers told Reuters that illegal imports could exceed 100t in 2026, against 20.4t in 2025 ([Kitco/Reuters](https://www.kitco.com/news/off-the-wire/2026-06-09/indias-gold-tariff-hike-fuels-smuggling-revival-squeezes-banks-and-0)).

### The rupee helped in 2025 but hurt in the crash week

The rupee fell about 5% in 2025, to 89.88, making it Asia's worst-performing currency ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/business/rupee-plunges-5-in-2025-becomes-asias-worst-performing-currency)). It hit a **record low of 96.9650 per dollar in May 2026** during the oil shock ([ThePrint](https://theprint.in/economy/rupee-slide-tests-rbis-multiple-defenses-as-record-low-nears/3065497/)). The RBI defended it in several ways: curbs on currency derivatives, a dollar window for oil refiners, and a 25bp repo hike to 5.50% on 7 Oct ([ThePrint](https://theprint.in/economy/rupee-slide-tests-rbis-multiple-defenses-as-record-low-nears/3065497/); [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/latest/economy/story/rupee-near-record-low-why-rbis-multiple-defences-are-losing-effectiveness-560381-2026-10-08)). In November, foreign investors bought nearly $6bn of Indian shares in ten days ([Finshots](https://finshots.in/archive/has-rbi-stabilised-rupee/)), and the rupee rose from 94.19 on 13 Nov to 92.62 on 20 Nov, its fifth straight gain ([Shillong Times](https://theshillongtimes.com/2026/11/21/rupee-closes-at-92-62-against-us-dollar/)). That 1.7% gain added roughly 1.5–2 percentage points to MCX gold's weekly fall of about 11% *(calc.)*. People who bought between May and October lost on both counts: they paid the highest duty, and the rupee then strengthened against them.

### Demand: volumes near six-year lows, spending at records

Indian dealer prices tracked the cycle. Dealers offered discounts of up to $61/oz during the January run ([Business Standard/Reuters](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/commodities/india-china-flip-to-gold-premiums-as-prices-retreat-from-record-highs-126010200403_1.html)). On duty-hike day the discount hit a record above $200 (up to $207), as dealers dumped stock on which the old duty had been paid ([Kitco/Reuters](https://www.kitco.com/news/off-the-wire/2026-05-13/gold-discounts-india-breach-200ounce-record-amid-profit-taking)). Discounts averaged $66 in September and narrowed to $5 by 12 Nov as festive buying returned ([WGC](https://www.gold.org/goldhub/gold-focus/2026/11/india-gold-market-update-gold-demand-gets-festive-boost)).

Official gold imports fell. October imports were $5.51bn, down 62.57% from a year earlier but more than three times September's figure. April–October imports fell 40%, to $24.78bn ([ANI via inkl](https://www.inkl.com/news/indias-gold-imports-plunge-62-57-to-5-51-billion-in-october-as-prices-fall-below-4-000-oz); [Lapaas Voice](https://lapaasvoice.com/india-gold-imports-fall-40-to-24-78-billion-in-april-october-2026)).

The WGC's India data show volumes falling while spending rose. Q2 demand was 130.9t (-6%), but its value was a record ₹1,98,100 crore (+50%) ([WGC](https://www.gold.org/goldhub/research/gold-demand-trends/gold-demand-trends-india-focus-q2-2026)). In Q3, demand was 162.5t (-29%); jewellery was 99.9t (-20%), and bars and coins were 55t (-40%). The WGC cut its 2026 India forecast to **625–675t, possibly a six-year low** ([WGC](https://www.gold.org/goldhub/research/gold-demand-trends/gold-demand-trends-india-focus-q3-2026); [GJEPC](https://gjepc.org/solitaire/indias-gold-demand-falls-29-in-q3-as-high-prices-restrain-buying/)). Dhanteras 2026 (6 Nov) sales were about ₹60,000 crore, according to the GJC (the jewellers' trade body): value up 15–20%, volume roughly flat ([News Today](https://newstodaynet.com/2026/11/07/dhanteras-gold-sales-hit-rs-60000-crore/)). Lower prices are now drawing wedding-season buyers back into shops in Rajkot and Delhi ([Punjab Kesari/IANS](https://english.punjabkesari.com/business/gold-silver-price-correction-spurs-jewellery-demand-ahead-of-wedding-season)). That should soften further falls, but it is not a floor.

## 2026 ETF buyers, gold-loan borrowers and MCX traders carry India's losses

**Indian retail money bought the top.** Gold ETFs took in a record **₹24,040 crore in January 2026**. It was the first month on record in which gold ETF inflows beat inflows into equity mutual funds ([Ventura Securities](https://www.venturasecurities.com/news/mutual-funds/amfi-data-2026-gold-rush-moment-as-gold-etf-inflows-surpass-equity-mutual-fund-inflows-in-january-for-the-first-time/)). In May, as the duty hike and the PM's appeal hit, gold ETFs saw their first outflow in over a year, about ₹725 crore ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/bt-tv/market-today/video/amfi-data-may-2026-record-gold-etf-outflows-check-where-is-investors-money-going-536023-2026-06-11)). Inflows then resumed: ₹4,450.61 crore in September and ₹3,474.03 crore in October ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/personal-finance/story/gold-etf-inflows-decline-22-to-rs3470-crore-in-october-are-investors-turning-cautious-on-gold-566904-2026-11-11)). January–October inflows totalled about ₹49,399 crore, more than in all of 2025 ([5paisa](https://www.5paisa.com/blog/gold-etf-inflows-cross-rs-49000-crore-first-10-months-2026)). The WGC estimates a further ₹2,450 crore came in during 1–11 Nov ([WGC](https://www.gold.org/goldhub/gold-focus/2026/11/india-gold-market-update-gold-demand-gets-festive-boost)).

Silver ETFs took in ₹3,092.22 crore in October, up 56.6% on September, even though their assets fell 14% to about ₹70,221 crore ([AMFI](https://www.amfiindia.com/uploads/AMFI_Monthly_Note_October2026_cd03bc1103.pdf); [Upstox](https://upstox.com/news/personal-finance/mutual-funds/amfi-october-2026-data-8-key-takeaways-on-equity-mf-inflows-debt-funds-gold-and-silver-et-fs/article-203285/)). Gold ETF assets fell from ₹1,86,310 crore to ₹1,79,894 crore in October despite the inflows. The number of gold ETF investor accounts (folios) reached 1.27 crore, up from about 1.02 crore in December 2025 ([Indmoney](https://www.indmoney.com/blog/mutual-funds/gold-etf-inflows-increases-why-are-investors-rushing-to-buy)). So far more Indians are sitting on gold losses than in any earlier Indian gold correction *(inference)*. Indian retail is averaging down at a time when global silver ETFs are shedding metal.

**Policy also restricted how money could flow in.** From about 15–16 Oct, around 17 fund houses, including HDFC, ICICI Prudential, Tata, Invesco and Kotak, stopped accepting new money into gold ETF fund-of-funds: no fresh lump sums, no new SIPs or STPs, and no switch-ins. Existing SIPs continue, and ETFs traded on the exchange were not restricted ([Upstox](https://upstox.com/news/personal-finance/mutual-funds/gold-fund-si-ps-and-st-ps-suspended-what-icici-prudential-hdfc-tata-invesco-mf-and-others-said/article-202078/); [Moneylife](https://www.moneylife.in/article/mutual-funds-halt-gold-etf-and-fof-inflows-ahead-of-festive-season-as-rupee-stays-under-pressure/81972.html)). Reports link the move to pressure on the rupee and efforts to curb imports ([Value Research](https://www.valueresearchonline.com/learn/savings/why-gold-prices-falling-india-four-factors-november-2026/)). There is an earlier warning on ETF pricing too. In October 2025, during the London squeeze, silver ETFs traded 5–12% above the value of the silver they held ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/personal-finance/paying-too-much-silver-etfs-look-shiny-but-mind-the-5-12-premium-risk-125101300184_1.html)), and Kotak paused lump-sum investment into its silver FoF ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/kotak-mutual-fund-suspends-lumpsum-investments-in-silver-etf-fund-125101000120_1.html)). Always compare an ETF's market price with its iNAV (the live value of its holdings) before buying.

**Gold loans are the hidden pressure point for households.** Outstanding gold loans were about **₹3.74 trillion in September 2026, up roughly 60–66% from a year earlier**. That is much faster than the ~27% rise in gold prices over the same period **[secondary sources]** ([India Fintech Substack](https://indiafintech.substack.com/p/how-india-lends-q2-fy27-the-monthly); [Lapaas Voice](https://lapaasvoice.com/rbi-steps-up-monitoring-of-gold-loan-portfolios-as-falling-prices-push-ltv-ratios-above-100)). New RBI rules took effect on 1 Apr 2026. They cap the loan-to-value (LTV) ratio at 85% for loans up to ₹2.5 lakh, 80% for ₹2.5–5 lakh and 75% above ₹5 lakh, and limit bullet loans (repaid in one go at the end) to 12 months ([Upstox](https://upstox.com/learning-center/personal-finance/rbi-gold-loan-rules-2026/article-1545/)). A loan made at 85% LTV goes above 100% LTV after a price fall of about 15% *(calc.)*. On 20 Nov, IBJA gold was about 17% below its end-September level. The RBI has reportedly stepped up monitoring of loan books where LTV has gone above 100% **[secondary, citing Moneycontrol]** ([Lapaas Voice](https://lapaasvoice.com/rbi-steps-up-monitoring-of-gold-loan-portfolios-as-falling-prices-push-ltv-ratios-above-100)). At Muthoot, Stage 2 loans (loans showing signs of stress) jumped to about ₹23,467 crore from ₹1,876 crore a quarter earlier. At Manappuram, the Stage 2 share rose from about 0.5% to 5% ([India Fintech Substack](https://indiafintech.substack.com/p/how-india-lends-q2-fy27-the-monthly)). Both stocks fell about 5% on 18 Nov, and Muthoot hit a 52-week low of ₹2,305 ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/gold-financiers-under-pressure-muthoot-manappuram-fall-5-here-s-why-126111800267_1.html)). The sources do not separate the effect of the price fall from the new 12-month bullet-loan rule. Borrowers who took maximum-LTV loans in September–October face demands to add more gold or repay part of the loan, and the risk of their gold being auctioned if they cannot pay.

**SGBs show that long-term holders are still winning.** No new Sovereign Gold Bond has been issued since February 2024 ([Finowings](https://www.finowings.com/Investment/gold-bond-scheme); [GoldenPi](https://goldenpi.com/blog/bond-news/sovereign-gold-bond-scheme-discontinued-for-new-issues/)). For the 25 Nov early redemption of 2021-22 Series I, the RBI set a price of ₹12,612, about **+167%** over the issue price ([Angel One](https://www.angelone.in/news/personal-finance/sgb-2021-22-series-i-delivers-166-81-return-as-rbi-opens-premature-redemption-on-november-25-2026)). That is 6.6% below the ₹13,499 set one week earlier for Series VIII *(calc.)*, so the crash week shows up in a single number. Secondary sources say that from 1 Apr 2026 the capital-gains tax exemption applies only to original subscribers who hold until maturity. Buyers on the exchange, and possibly anyone redeeming early, would then pay 12.5% long-term capital gains tax **[unverified against the Finance Act 2026]** ([ArihantPlus](https://www.arihantplus.com/blogs/gold/gold-silver-tax-rules-after-budget-2026); [Calcguru](https://calcguru.in/sovereign-gold-sovereign-gold-bond-holder-options-have-stopped-what-an-existing-holder-should-do/)).

## Playbook: set a target allocation, buy in stages, avoid leverage

What follows is not personalised advice. It sets out what the cited evidence supports for a typical long-term retail investor. Anyone with specific needs should consult a SEBI-registered adviser. Two facts that justify holding some gold have survived the crash. Central banks are buying at a record pace. And in past slumps, the falling rupee has cushioned Indian buyers: in 2013 gold fell about 28% in dollars but only about 3% in rupees ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/article/markets/gold-prices-finally-fell-in-2013-113122500530_1.html)). The crash has also confirmed that silver is a volatile, partly industrial bet.

### Allocation ranges, and what each type of investor can reasonably do

Indian financial planners broadly recommend keeping **5–10% of a portfolio in gold, up to 15%**, as a stabiliser rather than a source of returns. This advice was given before the crash *[view]* ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/personal-finance/equity-vs-gold-vs-silver-how-investors-should-balance-portfolios-for-2026-126010200111_1.html); [BusinessToday](https://www.businesstoday.in/personal-finance/investment/story/2026-portfolio-blueprint-where-to-invest-after-gold-and-silvers-blockbuster-2025-517931-2026-02-25)). Mirae Asset treats silver as a tactical holding rather than a core one ([Cafemutual](https://cafemutual.com/news/industry/36629-gold-and-silver-outlook-2026)). The research notes put silver at about **0–3%** of a portfolio, within a combined gold-and-silver share of about 5–10% *(inference)*.

Institutional funds sit within that range. DSP's Multi Asset Omni FoF held about 10% in gold and no silver, against a benchmark with 20% in the two metals combined. On 18 Nov it said it would add 0.5% to gold from around $3,850, and 0.5% to silver from just below $47 and again at every further $2 fall. DSP called gold 15% and silver 28% below fair value *[view]* ([BusinessToday](https://www.businesstoday.in/personal-finance/story/gold-at-15-discount-silver-at-28-dsp-mutual-fund-explains-why-it-is-buying-precious-metals-now-568288-2026-11-19); [Upstox](https://upstox.com/news/personal-finance/mutual-funds/why-dsp-multi-asset-omni-fo-f-is-increasing-gold-and-silver-allocation-amid-2026-price-crash/article-203792/)). The same fund house was "cautious on gold, bullish on silver" in October 2025 ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/personal-finance/cautious-on-gold-bullish-on-silver-in-2026-what-dsp-advises-investors-125100800161_1.html)), the opposite of how things turned out. ### Mistakes the 2026 crash has already punished

The first and most expensive mistake is **selling in a panic at the low**. It locks in a loss that the rupee and the duty have already softened, and for recent buyers who still have a gain it can trigger slab-rate tax as well. The better question is "Am I above or below my target allocation?" The second is **averaging down with leverage on MCX**: in 2026 the exchanges raised margins exactly when prices were falling, which forced traders out whatever their long-term view. The third is **buying the dip in one lump sum**, when history shows a 30% fall can become 45% and last for years. The fourth is **overweighting silver because it "fell more" or "looks cheap"**. Its bigger fall reflects bigger risk, and the shortage story behind it has weakened.

The rest are quieter but just as costly. Buyers lose money by **treating jewellery or app-based digital gold as an investment** instead of using regulated ETFs or FoFs, and by **forgetting the jewellery they already own** when deciding how much gold to hold. Many **rely on confident forecasts**, though 2026 punished them: in January ING said silver's "downside might be limited" ([ING](https://think.ing.com/articles/trump-puts-silver-tariffs-on-hold)), and on 18 Nov a pv magazine headline said silver had found "a $50 floor" when it was already trading near $45 ([pv magazine](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2026/11/18/silver-prices-find-50-floor-as-solar-industry-seeks-to-accelerate-shift-toward-copper/)). Others **assume the 15% duty is permanent**, **buy SGBs on the exchange expecting a tax-free maturity**, or **borrow against gold at the maximum LTV near a price peak**. Each of these lost money in 2026.

## Conclusion

2026 showed that gold is first an interest-rate asset and only second a war hedge. When war means more expensive oil, higher inflation and Fed hikes, gold falls. The variable that will decide the next move is the US real yield, on the path set by the 9 Dec Fed meeting, not news from West Asia. For Indian investors there is a second variable of almost equal weight: New Delhi's policy. The duty hike, the PM's appeal, the halt to new money into gold FoFs and the silver import licences moved rupee prices nearly as much as the dollar did. The 15% duty, which softened this fall, is now the biggest India-specific risk, because a single Budget decision could reverse it.

The other lesson concerns how people held gold, not gold itself. The real damage fell on borrowed money (MCX positions, maximum-LTV gold loans) and on buyers who arrived late (January 2026 ETF inflows and silver bought above $100). Patient holders are still well ahead, from end-2024 buyers up about 60% to 2020–21 SGB holders up about 160%. For the video, the honest message is not "buy the dip" or "gold is finished". It is: keep a target allocation, buy only in stages, avoid leverage, and treat silver as a small speculation whose bull case now depends on investors returning, not on a shortage.
