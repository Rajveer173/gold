# Fed Hikes and Forced Selling Broke Bullion

Gold and silver fell from their January 2026 records for one main reason. The US Federal Reserve swung from cutting interest rates to raising them, and a market full of borrowed money was forced to sell into that swing. Spot gold has fallen about **37%**, from an intraday record of **$5,589.38 (28 Jan, US time)** to a **20 Nov low of $3,513.54**, and it traded near **$3,520 on 25 Nov**, a 14-month low. Silver has fallen about **67%**, from **$121.64** to **$40.10 on 25 Nov**, its lowest in more than 14 months. The fall came in six legs. First came the 30 Jan crash after Kevin Warsh was named as the next Fed chair and exchanges raised margins. Then came a −12% March after the US-Israeli war on Iran began on 28 Feb, a mid-year slide below $4,000 on 24 Jun, the Fed's first hike since 2023 on 16 Sep, and a November rout. In November markets priced a December hike at about 97%, real yields stood near 24-year highs, and an AI-stock crash led by Nvidia's 14% fall forced investors to sell gold for cash. Silver fell about twice as hard. Its London shortage and US tariff premiums disappeared, solar makers cut silver use, and speculators left. Central banks kept buying and did not cause the fall. Indians lost less. MCX gold is about **33% below its ₹1,80,501 peak**, and IBJA 24K gold is about **9% down for 2026**, against about 19% in dollars. The main reason is the **13 May import-duty hike from 6% to 15%**, which lifted domestic prices by about 8.5%. That cushion would reverse by about 7.8% if the 1 Feb 2027 Budget cut the duty back. The losses fall mainly on three groups. The first is 2026 buyers: a record ₹24,040 crore went into gold ETFs in January alone. The second is leveraged MCX traders. The third is borrowers with gold loans near the maximum loan-to-value (LTV) ratio. Buyers from before 2025 and original 2020–21 Sovereign Gold Bond holders are still well in profit. The evidence supports a calm, rules-based response: keep a target allocation (gold about 5–10%, silver a small tactical slice), add only in stages through regulated ETFs, avoid leverage, and watch the 25 Nov PCE print, the 9 Dec Fed decision, real yields, the rupee and the duty decision in the 1 Feb 2027 Budget. This edition replaces the earlier brief. The data verification table near the end lists each figure that was disputed and how it was settled.

### The Fed went from cutting to hiking, and real yields rose to the highest since 2002

Timing is the strongest evidence. The 30 Jan crash followed news that President Trump would nominate Kevin Warsh as Fed chair. Markets read him as hawkish and pro-dollar, which undercut the debasement story ([Finviz](https://finviz.com/news/293188/trumps-fed-choice-kevin-warsh-sparks-dollar-surge-metals-crash)). Warsh's first meeting, on 16–17 Jun, was a "hawkish hold" at 3.50–3.75%. That day he set up five internal task forces, with the balance sheet as the headline item ([Yahoo/AFP](https://uk.finance.yahoo.com/news/us-federal-kicks-off-first-141534974.html); [Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/fed-task-force-balance-sheet-warsh/)). At Jackson Hole in late August he warned the Fed would "have work to do" if underlying inflation did not return to 2%. Gold fell about 2.75% on the day and erased much of an August rally of about 14% ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/31/jackson-hole-fed-chair-kevin-warsh-hawkish-rate-hikes-analysts.html)). On **16 Sep the Fed raised rates 25 basis points (bp) to 3.75–4.00%**, its first hike since 2023, by a 12–0 vote ([Kitco](https://www.kitco.com/news/article/2026-09-16/gold-price-drops-4310oz-fed-votes-12-0-favor-25-bps-rate-hike-16-18)). On **28 Oct it held by 10–2**. **Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack dissented in favour of a hike** ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/28/fed-rate-decision-warsh-takeaways.html); [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-28/fed-holds-rates-says-more-data-will-help-next-decision)). The tightening came through the policy rate, not through quantitative tightening (QT, shrinking the Fed's bond holdings). QT ended on 1 Dec 2025, and Warsh ruled out fast QT at Sintra in early July ([VG Markets](https://www.vgmarkets.com/en/news-detail/no-fast-qt-warsh-rules-out-aggressive-fed-balance-sheet-202607020303.html)). The research found no direct Warsh statement about gold.

November brought three blows in a row. October CPI, released on 10 Nov, rose 0.4% month on month against 0.3% expected, with headline inflation near 4% ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/cpi_11102026.htm)). The 18 Nov minutes said "almost all participants" saw further hikes as likely appropriate. The same day Governor Christopher Waller called a 50bp December hike "probably necessary" ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/18/fed-minutes-october-2026.html); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/18/federal-reserve-governor-christopher-waller.html)). CME FedWatch odds of a December hike went from 88.8% after the CPI release to **97.4% for 25bp and 2.6% for 50bp on 24 Nov**. Pricing for January implied a 67.9% chance of a cumulative 50bp of hikes ([Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/economy-news/fed-rate-hike-odds-in-december-at-88-after-hot-cpi-cme-fedwatch-5017585); [Longbridge](https://longbridge.com/en/news/306688134)). The decision is due on **9 Dec** (the meeting runs 8–9 Dec). A 25bp hike is almost fully priced, so the real downside risk is a 50bp move or a signal of more hikes in 2027.

The channel to gold is the **real yield**, the return on an inflation-protected US government bond. The 10-year TIPS yield rose from 1.72% on 27 Feb to 2.95% on 5 Oct ([Macrotrends](https://www.macrotrends.net/3010/10-year-tips-yield)). A 19 Nov auction cleared at **2.903%, the highest for that maturity since July 2002** ([TIPSWatch](https://tipswatch.com/2026/11/19/10-year-tips-auction-gets-real-yield-of-2-903-highest-in-24-years/)). When investors can lock in nearly 3% above inflation at no risk, holding gold, which pays nothing, becomes expensive. The nominal 10-year yield closed at **5.07% on 18 Nov, 5.04% on 19 Nov and 4.92% on 20 Nov**, and the 10-year TIPS yield was **2.77% on 20 Nov** ([FRED DGS10](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/dgs10); [FRED DFII10](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DFII10)). The "about 5.1%" in Indian reports reflects 18–19 Nov. By 24 Nov the nominal yield had eased to about 4.90% ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/amp/2026/11/24/treasury-yields-bonds-inflation.html)). The banks blamed the same channel when they cut forecasts. JPMorgan pointed in July to "a sharp re-emergence of gold's sensitivity to real interest rates" *[view]* ([TheStreet](https://www.thestreet.com/investing/jpmorgan-tweaks-gold-price-target-as-fed-risks-return)). Goldman cut its forecast twice in November, citing a "hawkish Fed shift" *[view]* ([Yahoo Finance](https://sg.finance.yahoo.com/news/goldman-sachs-revamps-2027-gold-161700634.html)).

### Leverage turned each Fed shock into a crash

Borrowed money explains why the falls were so violent. CME (the US futures exchange operator) raised margins as the rally peaked and broke. The verifiable sequence starts with dollar-based silver increases in late December and early January ([AP via Yahoo](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/surging-silver-gold-slide-cme-160127327.html)), followed by a switch to margins set as a percentage of contract value around 13 Jan. On **28 Jan** silver went from 9% to 11% ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-01-28/cme-raises-silver-margins-as-prices-smash-records-in-wild-rally)). A statement on **Friday 30 Jan** raised gold from 6% to 8% and silver from 11% to 15%, effective after the 2 Feb close ([MINING.COM/Reuters](https://www.mining.com/web/cme-raises-gold-silver-margins-after-historic-price-plunge/); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/02/02/gold-silver-fall-further-as-cme-margin-hike-stokes-selling.html)). Around **5–6 Feb** silver went from 15% to 18% ([MINING.COM](https://www.mining.com/web/cme-hikes-precious-metal-margins-again-after-price-swings/)). The widely shared "five hikes in nine days" count comes from a single blog and could not be verified. On screen, say "CME raised margins repeatedly in late January and early February". India followed. MCX set margins at 20% for gold and 25% for silver from 1 Feb and added extra margins on 5–6 Feb. ICICI Securities estimated that the effective silver margin rose from about 15% to **72%** until the extras were withdrawn on 19 Feb ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/markets/stocks/story/mcx-shares-additional-margin-on-gold-silver-futures-withdrawn-what-icici-sec-says-516865-2026-02-19); [MCX circular](https://www.mcxindia.com/docs/default-source/circulars/english/2026/january/mcxmcxccl-060-2026-withdrawal-of-additional-margin-in-gold-and-silver.pdf?sfvrsn=5de03b9e_0)).

In March, the WGC said investors were "selling what they could rather than what they wanted to be rid of", which describes selling to raise cash. Gold ETFs lost **$12bn (84t)** that month, almost all of it in North America ([WGC](https://www.gold.org/goldhub/research/gold-market-commentary-march-2026)). November repeated the pattern. In the week to 20 Nov, global gold ETFs lost **20.4t (about $2.2bn), the most since June**. North America accounted for 11.8t, Europe 6.4t and Asia 2.2t ([FXEmpire](https://www.fxempire.com/forecasts/article/gold-price-forecast-etf-outflows-threaten-golds-sharp-recovery-1646912); [BeInCrypto](https://beincrypto.com/gold-etf-outflows-biggest-since-june/)). SPDR GLD holdings fell to 1,034.4t ([Longbridge](https://longbridge.com/en/news/306347445)). ING noted that gold "kept falling ... even as Treasury yields eased and the dollar's momentum cooled, a sign that liquidation, not rates alone, was doing the damage" *[view]* ([investinglive](https://investinglive.com/commodities/gold-sinks-9-in-a-week-as-margin-calls-and-fed-hike-bets-override-its-haven-role/)). North American selling in the same week as the US tech rout fits the margin-call story. But ETF flows cannot prove margin calls, and no broker data quantifies them, so the link is strong but circumstantial.

CME's latest move came *after* the slide. **CME Clearing advisory 26-389, dated 23 Nov**, raised COMEX gold margins from 5% to 6% of contract value (+20%) and silver from 8% to 9% (+12.5%), effective after the 24 Nov close. CME described it as a routine volatility review ([Goldreporter](https://www.goldreporter.de/cme-erhoeht-margins-gold-silber-futures/goldmarkt/263307/); [Invezz](https://invezz.com/sg/news/2026/11/24/gold-prices-fall-as-cme-raises-margins-by-20percent-is-another-crash-coming/)). Gold margins were 8% in February but 5% before this notice, so CME must have cut them during the year. November's 6% is therefore still below the February peak *(inference)*. The washout may not be complete. On 17 Nov, managed-money net longs in COMEX gold (speculative funds' net bets on higher prices) were 102,942 contracts, still near their five-year median ([Businessquant](https://businessquant.com/economic-data/cotgoldmm)). Goldman warned that ETF holders could capitulate *[view]* ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/commodities/articles/goldman-lowers-end-gold-price-081423381.html)).

### The Iran war hurt gold because oil meant rate hikes

The US-Israeli strikes on Iran began on **Saturday 28 Feb 2026**, and Iran closed the Strait of Hormuz ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Iran_war); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/03/02/gold-climbs-as-us-israel-strikes-on-iran-spark-safe-haven-demand.html)). On the first trading day, 2 Mar, gold rose only modestly: spot was +0.4% at $5,297.31 and futures settled +1.2%. Finance Magnates put the opening gain at nearly 2% **[conflict]** ([Finance Magnates](https://www.financemagnates.com/trending/gold-price-tests-5400-oil-jumps-13-as-strait-of-hormuz-shuts-iran-war-rocks-markets/)). Then gold slid. By 12 Mar, CNBC was asking why gold had not moved ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/03/12/gold-iran-conflict-where-next-markets.html)). It finished March **−12% at $4,608, its weakest month since June 2013** on the WGC's LBMA month-end basis ([WGC](https://www.gold.org/goldhub/research/gold-market-commentary-march-2026)). The competing "worst since 2008" headlines from CNBC and Trading Economics were spot-based readings published on 31 Mar before the month closed, when spot was near $4,570 ([Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/commodity/gold/news/537895)). The war created forced sellers among central banks too. Central banks were net sellers of **30t** in March (37t bought, 66t sold), led by **Turkey (about −60t)**. Reports of Russia's sales differ: 6t in WGC statistics against 16t in a WGC statement **[conflict]** ([Kitco](https://www.kitco.com/news/article/2026-05-05/central-banks-were-net-sellers-gold-march-30-tonnes-outflows-wgc); [IndexBox](https://www.indexbox.io/blog/central-banks-post-net-gold-sales-of-30-tonnes-in-march-2026/)).

The ceasefires proved the mechanism. When the US and Iran agreed a two-week truce on 8 Apr, gold *rose* almost 2% to $4,790 because inflation fears eased ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/conormurray/2026/04/08/gold-and-silver-hit-3-week-highs-after-us-iran-ceasefire-agreement/)). Brent futures peaked at **$126.41 on 30 Apr**, during the US naval blockade ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/04/30/energy/oil-prices-iran-war-wartime-high-blockade-hnk)). A June memorandum eased the blockade, but the ceasefire collapsed on 8 Jul after attacks on shipping ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Iran_war_ceasefire)). The exact June date and the first collapse are disputed. The war has **not ended**. Al Jazeera calls the current state "no war, no peace" ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/12/no-war-no-peace-can-iran-break-the-deadlock-with-the-us)). On 24 Nov Iran's army chief ordered Hormuz closed "further day by day", while Qatar pushed to restart talks ([Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/2026/11/24/iran-orders-hormuz-closed-further-day-by-day-as-qatar-seeks-to-revive-peace-talks)). In November, Brent fell from $117.64 (30 Oct) to below $89 (23 Nov) **[1 source]** ([House of Saud](https://houseofsaud.com/brent-third-weekly-loss-nov23/)), and gold fell with it. By then the Fed and liquidation dominated, so cheaper oil did not rescue gold. The research found no Russia–Ukraine peace deal in 2026. Peace headlines hit gold in late 2025, but in 2026 ceasefire news sometimes *helped* gold, because lower oil meant fewer hikes. Modern Diplomacy's one-liner fits the video: "Gold's slide is a bet on the Fed, not on peace. Peace may be what rescues it" *[view]* ([Modern Diplomacy](https://moderndiplomacy.eu/2026/11/22/gold-sell-off-2026-fed-rate-hikes-iran-war-central-banks/)).

### A firmer dollar and weak buying mattered only at the margin

The dollar index rose from about 100.5 in June to 103.4–103.65 in mid-November, the highest since April 2025 ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/18/gold-slides-to-over-one-year-low-on-firm-dollar-us-rate-outlook.html)). It touched 104.54 on 23 Nov ([Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/indices/usdollar-historical-data)). That is roughly +3%, against gold's −37% *(calc.)*. On 24 Nov gold fell even as the dollar eased, because bets on hikes dominated ([FXStreet](https://www.fxstreet.com/news/gold-slides-despite-weaker-us-dollar-as-fed-hike-bets-dominate-202611241748)). Physical buying weakened *before* the crash, so it removed a cushion rather than causing the fall. In WGC's Q3 data, global jewellery demand fell 17% and bars and coins fell 19%. Total demand slipped only 1%, because ETFs and central banks filled the gap ([WGC](https://www.gold.org/goldhub/research/gold-demand-trends/gold-demand-trends-q3-2026)).

Central banks bought harder as prices fell. It did not set the world price.

### Where analysts disagree

Four disagreements are live, and each involves views rather than settled facts. The first is **what drove November**. ING stresses liquidation, while Goldman and JPMorgan stress the Fed and real rates ([Discovery Alert](https://discoveryalert.com/analysis/gold-silver-sell-off-evidence-november-2026/)). The second is **October**. The WGC's model says falling yields *supported* gold that month, so it blames "asset rotation, de-grossing and Fed rate-path repricing" instead ([WGC](https://www.gold.org/goldhub/research/gold-market-commentary-october-2026)). The third is **whether the bottom is near**. UBS calls the sell-off "positioning-led, not structural", and the WGC says gold "may be nearing an interim bottom" ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/commodities/articles/gold-rebounds-traders-pare-bets-002803539.html); [newsd.in](https://newsd.in/gold-market-outlook-wgc-sees-possible-bottom-after-sharp-2026-price-correction/)). Saxo's Ole Hansen warns the "brutal sell-off may not be over" ([TheStreet](https://thestreet.com/investing/ole-hansen-gold-sell-off)). The fourth is **the cause of the 30 Jan silver crash**: Warsh and the dollar, or CME margin mechanics.

## Silver fell twice as far because its scarcity story fell apart

Silver's −67%, against gold's −37%, reflects three supports that only silver had, and all three collapsed on top of the shared rate shock. From 30 Oct to 20 Nov silver fell 15.4%, exactly in line with copper, while gold fell 8.8%. In that leg silver traded like an industrial metal, not like money ([BullionVault](https://www.bullionvault.com/gold-news/gold-price-news/gold-silver-crash-rally-112320261)).

**The shortage premium disappeared.** In October 2025 London ran short of freely available silver. One-month lease rates (the cost of borrowing physical metal) hit about 39%, after some 225 Moz had moved to New York vaults on US tariff fears ([FinancialContent](https://markets.financialcontent.com/stocks/article/marketminute-2025-10-10-silvers-historic-squeeze-deepens-london-market-in-chaos-as-prices-soar-past-50); [Kitco](https://www.kitco.com/opinion/2026-04-23/londons-silver-running-out-room)). By end-September 2026 London vaults held **932 Moz, the most in nearly four years** ([Kitco](https://www.kitco.com/news/article/2026-10-29/silver-could-see-further-losses-against-gold-physical-market-weakens-metals)).

**The tariff and price-floor premium never materialised.** Silver joined the US Critical Minerals List in November 2025 ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/11/07/2025-19813/final-2025-list-of-critical-minerals)). **Proclamation 11001 of 14 Jan 2026 imposed no Section 232 tariff**. It chose negotiations and a possible import price floor instead, and gave Commerce 180 days to report ([Thompson Hine](https://www.thompsonhine.com/insights/no-section-232-tariff-now-on-processed-critical-minerals-and-their-derivative-products/); [ING](https://think.ing.com/articles/trump-puts-silver-tariffs-on-hold)). The research found no public outcome of the July report. A November analysis says the listing produced no tariff, no stockpile and no price floor **[1 source]** ([Discovery Alert](https://discoveryalert.com/analysis/silver-critical-mineral-stockpile-price-floor-november-2026/)). China's silver-export licensing, in force from 1 Jan 2026 with 44 licensed exporters, is confirmed. FXStreet called its impact "overstated" *[view]* ([FXStreet](https://www.fxstreet.com/analysis/impact-of-new-chinese-silver-export-regulations-overstated-202601152204)).

**High prices destroyed demand.** The Silver Institute's 12 Nov interim review cut the 2026 deficit to **35 Moz, the narrowest in six years**, from 46.3 Moz in April. Total demand is down 5% to 1.08bn oz ([Silver Institute](https://silverinstitute.org/silver-market-set-for-sixth-consecutive-annual-deficit-in-2026/)). Silver used in solar panels was forecast to fall about 19%, to about 151 Moz, the biggest drop on record for silver's largest industrial use, as makers thrift silver and switch to copper ([IndexBox](https://www.indexbox.io/blog/silver-market-faces-sixth-consecutive-deficit-despite-solar-industry-cutting-usage/)). Industrial demand outside solar still grew 4.5%, so the electrification case is dented, not dead. Metals Focus expects a **2027 surplus**, while Bank of America expects a deficit *[views]* ([Kitco](https://www.kitco.com/news/article/2026-10-29/silver-could-see-further-losses-against-gold-physical-market-weakens-metals); [Finbold](https://finbold.com/banking-giant-sets-silver-price-for-2027/)).

**Positioning amplified every move.** On 30 Jan SLV lost 31% and leveraged silver ETFs lost more than 60% ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/01/30/silver-gold-fall-price-usd-dollar-fed-warsh-chair-trump-metals.html)). Silver coin sales at leading mints fell more than 80% between Q1 and Q2 ([Kitco](https://www.kitco.com/news/article/2026-10-29/silver-could-see-further-losses-against-gold-physical-market-weakens-metals)). Speculative net longs on COMEX shrank to just **about 1,280 contracts on 17 Nov** ([Business Quant](https://businessquant.com/economic-data/cotsilvermm)). Contrarians may read that as a sign the bottom is near. BullionVault calculates that silver lost **39% in 13 weeks to 24 Nov, its steepest since the Lehman crash of late 2008**. No other outlet has published that statistic, so attribute it on screen: "according to BullionVault…" ([BullionVault](https://www.bullionvault.com/gold-news/gold-price-news/gold-silver-fed-mistake-112420261)).

**India added its own twist.** **DGFT Notification 17/2026-27 of 16 May** moved silver bars from "Free" to "Restricted", and silver grain and powder followed in June ([StudyCafe](https://studycafe.in/dgft-tightens-silver-imports-key-silver-categories-shifted-from-free-to-restricted-421096.html); [WION](https://www.wionews.com/world/india-restricts-imports-of-two-types-of-silver-bars-with-immediate-effect-1778942317825)). May imports collapsed (33t by one count, 46.8t by another **[conflict]**) ([Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/business/indias-silver-imports-plummet-87-as-supply-is-hampered-by-import-restrictions/)). By July, domestic premiums were at a six-month high ([Kitco/Reuters](https://www.kitco.com/news/off-the-wire/2026-07-08/india-silver-import-curbs-create-shortages-push-premiums-six-month)). DGFT introduced an expedited approval procedure from 15 Oct. On **22 Oct Reuters reported**, citing four dealers, that permits had been issued for **about 6,500t of imports over six months**. Bloomberg reported up to 6,000t. This was not an official government figure. Dealer premiums fell to $0.45/oz from up to $2.50 a week earlier ([Business Recorder/Reuters](https://www.brecorder.com/news/40445452/india-permits-large-silver-imports-despite-weak-domestic-demand)). Indian silver ETFs fell about 4% the next day **[1 source]** ([Techyorker](https://techyorker.com/why-indian-silver-etfs-fell-up-to-4-after-the-6500-ton-import-approval-on-october-23-2026/)). Licensing restricts imports but does not ban them, so claims that imports were "halted completely" overstate it.

**History says silver slumps last years.** Silver peaked near $49.45 in January 1980 and fell to $10.80 on Silver Thursday, 27 Mar 1980 ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Silver-Thursday)). That day's fall is put at −50% or −33% depending on the opening price used **[conflict]** ([APMEX](https://learn.apmex.com/learning-guide/history/silver-thursday-the-hunt-brothers-scheme/); [Benzinga](https://benzinga.com/z/11424343)). By 1991 silver was near $3.5, a loss of roughly 90%+ *(calc.)*. That figure fits Kitco's "about 90%" better than the 75% some sites give. After its 2011 peak near $49.5, silver fell 72% to $13.71 by December 2015 **[1 source]** ([Kitco](https://www.kitco.com/opinion/2025-11-05/silver-high-where-was-it-1980-how-did-it-develop-2011-and-bear-market-looming)). It did not trade above $50 until **9 Oct 2025**, about 14.5 years later ([Mining.com](https://www.mining.com/silver-price-hits-50-for-first-time-since-1980/)). That is a sobering point for anyone who bought above $100 in January.

### The duty explains most of India's smaller loss, and could reverse it

Between 29 Jan and 25 Nov, MCX gold fell about 33% while spot gold fell 37%. For 2026 so far, IBJA gold is down about 9% against about 18.5% for spot gold *(calc.)*. The rupee explains only a small part of that gap: it closed at 91.99 on 29 Jan and 92.91 on 23 Nov. The duty explains most of it. The 1 Feb 2026 Budget **left the import duty unchanged at 6%** (5% basic customs duty + 1% AIDC, an agriculture cess) ([NewsBytes](https://www.newsbytesapp.com/news/business/union-budget-2026-no-change-in-gold-silver-import-duties/story); [A2Z Taxcorp](https://a2ztaxcorp.net/budget-2026-27-keeps-customs-duty-unchanged-for-gold-and-silver-bullions/)). The claim that it was cut to 5% is wrong. Then **Notifications 15/2026-Customs and 16/2026-Customs, dated 12 May 2026, raised the duty on gold and silver to 15%** (10% basic customs duty + 5% AIDC) from **13 May**. IGST of 3% applies on top ([A2Z Taxcorp](https://a2ztaxcorp.net/government-revises-customs-duty-structure-on-gold-silver-and-precious-metals-imports-sws-and-aidc-rates-rationalised-effective-from-may-13-2026/); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/05/13/india-hikes-bullion-import-duties-to-arrest-rupee-slide.html)). The hike came three days after the PM's appeal. Doré (unrefined bullion) is reportedly charged 14.35% **[1 source]**. It raises landed cost by 1.15/1.06, about **+8.5%** *(calc.)*, and MCX gold jumped 7.2% that day ([The Week](https://www.theweek.in/news/biz-tech/2026/05/13/india-gold-import-duty-hike.html)). The duty plus a rupee about 3% weaker than end-2025 accounts almost exactly for the gap: 0.815 × 1.085 × 1.034 ≈ 0.914, implying about −8.6% in rupees against −8.9% observed *(calc.)*. The current MCX price also matches import parity: $3,520 × ₹92.9 × 0.3215 × 1.15 ≈ ₹1,20,900, against ₹1,20,699 traded *(calc.)*.

CBIC cut the gold **tariff value**, the official reference price used to compute duty, to $1,245/10g from 14 Nov (Notification 91/2026-Customs (N.T.)) and to **$1,149/10g from 24 Nov** (Notification 96/2026-Customs (N.T.)). These cuts lower the duty paid per 10g in line with world prices. They do not change the 15% rate ([CorpLawUpdates](https://www.corplawupdates.in/updates/cbic-notification-96-2026-gold-tariff-value-cut); [taxguru](https://taxguru.in/custom-duty/cbic-revises-gold-import-tariff-24-november-2026.html)). The research found **no rollback** of the 15% rate as of 25 Nov. One explainer says a cut was "under consideration" **[1 source]** ([Insightful Trade](https://insightfultrade.com/gold-import-duty-in-india/)).

The cushion comes from policy, so policy can take it away. The July 2024 Budget cut the duty from 15% to 6% ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/business/banking/india-budget-2024-custom-duties-on-gold-silver-slashed-down-to-6-from-15-1.1721716047012)). A similar cut now would knock about **7.8%** off domestic prices overnight *(calc., 1.06/1.15)*. The hike also revived smuggling. The WGC estimates that unofficial inflows jumped more than 1,500% to about **50t in Q3** ([IBTimes India](https://www.ibtimes.co.in/indias-illegal-gold-imports-surge-over-1500-50-tonnes-higher-import-duty-fuels-unofficial-908097)). Domestic prices traded at a record discount to import parity, which the WGC linked to unofficial supply and old gold ([WGC India Q3](https://www.gold.org/goldhub/research/gold-demand-trends/gold-demand-trends-india-focus-q3-2026)).

### The rupee helped in 2025, then worked against holders in the crash weeks

The rupee fell about 5% in 2025, to 89.88, and hit a record low near **96.97 per dollar in May 2026** during the oil shock ([ThePrint](https://theprint.in/economy/rupee-slide-tests-rbis-multiple-defenses-as-record-low-nears/3065497/)). The RBI defended it in several ways, including a 25bp repo hike to 5.50% on 7 Oct ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/latest/economy/story/rupee-near-record-low-why-rbis-multiple-defences-are-losing-effectiveness-560381-2026-10-08)). In November, foreign investors poured money into Indian shares ([Finshots](https://finshots.in/archive/has-rbi-stabilised-rupee/)), and the rupee strengthened from 94.19 on 13 Nov to about 92.6–92.7 on 20 Nov. Sources differ on the 20 Nov close: 92.62 per PTI, 92.68 per Business Standard **[conflict]** ([Shillong Times](https://theshillongtimes.com/2026/11/21/rupee-closes-at-92-62-against-us-dollar/)). It slipped back to 92.91 on 23 Nov. That ~1.7% gain added roughly 1.5–2 percentage points to MCX gold's fall in the crash week *(calc.)*. Buyers between May and October lost on both counts: they paid the highest duty, and the rupee then strengthened against them.

**History shows the rupee has long been the Indian buyer's shock absorber.** In 2013 gold fell about 28% in dollars but only about 3% in rupees ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/article/markets/gold-prices-finally-fell-in-2013-113122500530_1.html)). Domestic gold set a record of **₹34,500/10g on 28 Aug 2013**, two years after the dollar peak, as the rupee crashed to about 68.8 ([Business Standard/PTI](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/article/pti-stories/gold-jumps-to-all-time-high-of-rs-34-500-on-weak-rupee-113082800348_1.html)). Even so, rupee gold went almost nowhere for years. Average prices moved from ₹31,050 in 2012 to ₹35,220 in 2019, about 1.8% a year ([BusinessToday](https://www.businesstoday.in/personal-finance/investment/story/gold-gave-zero-returns-for-8-years-ca-warns-investors-chasing-the-rs1-lakh-hype-474289-2025-05-01)). The 2013 rupee record was regained only in 2019, about six years later. In dollars, the 2011 high took about nine years to regain ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/article/markets/gold-trading-at-huge-discount-as-prices-hit-record-high-cross-rs-35k-mark-119071901143_1.html)). After 2020, the MCX record of ₹56,191 was regained on 13 Jan 2023, about 2.4 years later ([Business Standard/Reuters](https://bsppd.business-standard.com/article/markets/indian-gold-prices-hit-record-high-dampening-demand-say-dealers-123011300660_1.html)). Silver's rupee record before this cycle was **₹75,020/kg on 25 Apr 2011** ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/article/markets/2011-revisited-gold-and-silver-touch-all-time-highs-111122800117_1.html)). In 2026, the 15% duty has played the cushioning role the rupee played in 2013.

## Playbook: hold a target allocation, buy in stages, avoid leverage

What follows is not personalised advice. It sets out what the cited evidence supports for a typical long-term retail investor. Anyone with specific needs should consult a SEBI-registered adviser. Two facts that justify holding some gold have survived the crash. Central banks are buying faster as prices fall. And for Indian buyers, the rupee and duty have historically softened dollar crashes, though they have not removed long flat periods. The crash has also confirmed that silver is a volatile, partly industrial bet.

### Three scenarios for end-2027, and today's MCX price already sits in the bear case

The GST point needs to be stated precisely. A retail jewellery purchase attracts 3% GST on the whole bill, including making charges. The 5% rate applies only to job work, such as a karigar's labour on a jeweller's gold. Do not tell viewers that they pay "5% GST on making charges". One tax point is still open. Under the old Act, a short-term capital loss could be set off against both short- and long-term gains, a long-term loss only against long-term gains, and unabsorbed losses could be carried forward for eight years. The research could not confirm that the Income-tax Act, 2025 keeps these rules, so a chartered accountant should check them before broadcast. Panic selling also has a tax cost. Anyone who bought within the past 12 months (ETF) or 24 months (FoF or physical) and still has a gain pays tax on it at their slab rate.

### Mistakes the 2026 crash has already punished

The most expensive mistake is **selling in a panic at the low**. It locks in a loss that the rupee and duty have already softened, and recent buyers who still have a gain pay slab-rate tax as well. The better question is "Am I above or below my target allocation?" The second mistake is **averaging down with leverage on MCX**, because exchanges raised margins exactly when prices fell. The third is **buying the dip in one lump sum**, when history shows a 30% fall can become 45% and last for years. The fourth is **overweighting silver because it "fell more"**: its bigger fall reflects bigger risk, and its scarcity story has weakened. Quieter mistakes cost money too. Buyers have treated jewellery or app gold as an investment, and forgotten the jewellery they already own. They have trusted confident forecasts: on 18 Nov a pv magazine headline said silver had found "a $50 floor" while it traded near $45 ([pv magazine](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2026/11/18/silver-prices-find-50-floor-as-solar-industry-seeks-to-accelerate-shift-toward-copper/)). Others assumed the 15% duty was permanent, or bought SGBs on the exchange expecting a tax-free maturity that no longer exists. And some borrowed against gold at the maximum LTV near a price peak.

## Conclusion

The 2026 crash changes how Indian viewers should think about what drives gold. In dollars, gold behaved as a real-interest-rate asset that was also a source of cash in a leveraged market. War helped it only when war lowered the odds of rate hikes. In rupees, a third driver now carries almost equal weight: New Delhi's policy. The 15% duty, the silver licences, the PM's appeal and the SEBI-instructed halt on FoF inflows moved rupee prices by amounts comparable to the rupee itself. The rupee cushioned 2013. The duty cushioned 2026. A duty cushion can be removed in a single Budget speech. That makes 1 Feb 2027 as important for Indian holders as 9 Dec is for the world price.

The second new insight is about expectations, not direction. Before 25 Nov, today's MCX prices had already reached what most forecasters treated as the 2027 bear case, a year early. In rupee terms the bull case for silver still sits below its January price. The honest message for the video is therefore neither "buy the dip" nor "gold is finished". The damage fell on leverage and on late, lump-sum buying. Investors who hold a target allocation, buy in stages, keep SGBs to maturity, and treat silver as a small speculation now resting on investor demand rather than a shortage come out of 2026 bruised but not broken.
